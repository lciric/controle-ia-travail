This is a sharp question. In a restricted, static model—the KL-regularized (Gibbs) post-training equilibrium over *fixed* strata with fixed detection rates—it has exact answers, and both your argument and @testingthewaters's pushback show up as distinct terms in the same equations. The model does not reach your "generalise instead" worry (how training reshapes the stealthy behaviours and their detectability): it takes those as given.

Take three strata $\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{h}} \sqcup \Pi_{\mathrm{v}} \sqcup \Pi_{\mathrm{f}}$ (honest, visibly misaligned, stealthily misaligned; pretrained masses $p_{\mathrm{h}}, p_{\mathrm{v}}, p_{\mathrm{f}} > 0$), reward advantage $\Delta R > 0$ for misalignment, and an in-loop monitor with survival (false-negative) rates $0 \le \beta_{\mathrm{v}} < \beta_{\mathrm{f}} \le \beta_{\mathrm{h}} \le 1$. Compare, at KL inverse temperature $\eta > 0$:

- **Operator A (pure rejection, modelled as conditioning):** $\pi_{\mathrm{A}}^{(\eta)} \propto \beta\,e^{\eta R}\,\pi_0$.
- **Operator B (KL-regularized update with penalty $-C \le 0$):** $\pi_{\mathrm{B}}^{(\eta, C)} \propto \exp(\eta[\beta R - (1-\beta)C])\,\pi_0$.

**Theorem 1.**
1. *Conditional enrichment under A:* among misaligned runs, pure rejection raises expected survival by exactly $\operatorname{Var}(\beta)/\mathbb{E}[\beta] > 0$ (the Price/Robertson identity, i.e. Fisher's fundamental theorem for the trait "survival"). Under B this holds for two cheating strata of equal reward, not in general: with heterogeneous rewards, B can lower the cheaters' mean survival.
2. *Pure rejection, as modelled, cannot keep honesty dominant:* stealth beats honesty once $\Delta R > \frac{1}{\eta}(\log(p_{\mathrm{h}}/p_{\mathrm{f}}) + \log(\beta_{\mathrm{h}}/\beta_{\mathrm{f}})) \to 0$. Honesty collapses; the mass goes to cheating, but the stealthy share among cheaters only tends to $\beta_{\mathrm{f}}p_{\mathrm{f}}/(\beta_{\mathrm{v}}p_{\mathrm{v}}+\beta_{\mathrm{f}}p_{\mathrm{f}})$, which can be small. **Caveat:** this comes from modelling the filter as a reward bonus $\eta^{-1}\log\beta$ that fades with $\eta$. If rejection is repeated at every round ($\pi_T \propto \beta^T e^{T\eta_1 R}\pi_0$), the barrier $\eta_1\Delta R > \log(\beta_{\mathrm{h}}/\beta_{\mathrm{f}})$ does not vanish. So "rejection is fatal" is a property of the model of rejection, not a law.
3. *Penalty:* when stealth is partially caught ($\beta_{\mathrm{f}} < \beta_{\mathrm{h}}$), $C > 0$ keeps a non-vanishing barrier $\Delta R > \frac{\beta_{\mathrm{h}}-\beta_{\mathrm{f}}}{\beta_{\mathrm{f}}}C$. In a perfectly stealthy niche ($\beta_{\mathrm{f}} = \beta_{\mathrm{h}} = 1 > \beta_{\mathrm{v}} > 0$), B yields more absolute stealth than A iff $\eta(\Delta R + C) > \frac{\log(1/\beta_{\mathrm{v}})}{1-\beta_{\mathrm{v}}}$—but the stealth-to-honesty odds are identical under A and B, so exactly then B also yields more honesty and less total cheating. It is worse only on stealth.

**Theorem 2 (three escape mechanisms; not claimed exhaustive).**
- **(R1) Prior KL barrier & maintenance cost** *(exact criterion for honesty to dominate stealth, formalizing @testingthewaters)*: honesty dominates iff $\log(p_{\mathrm{h}}/p_{\mathrm{f}})$ exceeds the net exponent after subtracting maintenance costs $\delta_{\mathrm{maint}}$; under B, if $\beta_{\mathrm{f}}(\Delta R - \delta_{\mathrm{maint}}) < (\beta_{\mathrm{h}}-\beta_{\mathrm{f}})C$, stealth decays exponentially as $\eta \to \infty$.
- **(R2) Covariance, not placement**: for an audit monitor, pure rejection shifts audit survival on iso-reward cheaters by exactly $\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_0}(\beta_{\mathrm{loop}}, \beta_{\mathrm{audit}})/\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[\beta_{\mathrm{loop}}]$. Keeping the audit off-loop does not make that covariance $\le 0$—correlated detectors erode together. Under B, $\operatorname{Cov} \le 0$ is necessary but not sufficient; independence or negative association suffices. (Not training against your audit monitors is already the published recommendation—Baker et al. 2025, Korbak et al. 2025; the identity says it is not enough.)
- **(R3) Planted honeypots** *(extended contextual model)*: traps at total-variation distance $\le \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$ from ordinary episodes force any policy with ordinary cheating rate $\tau_{\mathrm{ord}} \ge \tau_{\min} > \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$ to hit traps at rate $\ge \tau_{\mathrm{ord}} - \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$, making it strictly worse than honesty once $C > C_{\mathrm{R3}}^*$, even with zero detection on ordinary episodes. Rare attackers are not excluded, so honesty is not guaranteed to be optimal.
