This is a sharp question. In one deliberately simple model — the KL-regularized (Gibbs) post-training equilibrium with fixed behaviour strata — it has exact answers, and both your argument and @testingthewaters's pushback show up as distinct terms in the same equations. The model does not answer the deeper part of your question (whether training against a detector teaches honesty or concealment): here the stealthy stratum and its detection rate are given, not learned.

Take a 3-regime policy space $\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{h}} \sqcup \Pi_{\mathrm{v}} \sqcup \Pi_{\mathrm{f}}$ (honest, visibly misaligned, stealthily misaligned; pretrained masses $p_{\mathrm{h}}, p_{\mathrm{v}}, p_{\mathrm{f}} > 0$), reward advantage $\Delta R > 0$ for misalignment, and an in-loop monitor with survival (false-negative) rates $0 \le \beta_{\mathrm{v}} < \beta_{\mathrm{f}} \le \beta_{\mathrm{h}} \le 1$. Compare two operators at KL inverse temperature $\eta > 0$:

- **Operator A (pure rejection, a modelling choice):** $\pi_{\mathrm{A}}^{(\eta)} \propto \beta\,e^{\eta R}\,\pi_0$. Equivalently, the KL-regularized optimum for the reward $R + \eta^{-1}\log\beta$: the filter acts as a bonus $\eta^{-1}\log\beta$ that fades as $\eta$ grows, by construction. This is one way to model "discard and try again"; it is not the only one (see the last point below).
- **Operator B (KL-regularized update with penalty $-C \le 0$):** $\pi_{\mathrm{B}}^{(\eta, C)} \propto \exp(\eta[\beta R - (1-\beta)C])\,\pi_0$.

**What holds exactly in this model.**
1. *Conditional enrichment under A:* among misaligned runs, rejection raises the mean survival rate by exactly $\operatorname{Var}(\beta)/\mathbb{E}[\beta]$ (a special case of Price's equation and of Robertson's selection identity). Under B, conditional enrichment holds in the two-stratum model with equal reward for visible and stealthy cheating; with heterogeneous rewards among cheaters it can fail.
2. *Pure rejection at high pressure:* stealth overtakes honesty once $\Delta R > \frac{1}{\eta}(\log\frac{p_{\mathrm{h}}}{p_{\mathrm{f}}} + \log\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{h}}}{\beta_{\mathrm{f}}}) \to 0$. Honesty collapses; the mass goes to the cheaters, and the stealthy share of cheaters tends to $\beta_{\mathrm{f}}p_{\mathrm{f}}/(\beta_{\mathrm{v}}p_{\mathrm{v}} + \beta_{\mathrm{f}}p_{\mathrm{f}})$, which can be small (mostly visible cheating). Halting without retry and without penalty ($C = 0$ in B) is also fatal: there $\pi_{\mathrm{B}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}) \to 1$.
3. *Penalty:* if stealth is partly caught ($\beta_{\mathrm{f}} < \beta_{\mathrm{h}}$), $C > 0$ leaves a barrier $\Delta R > \frac{\beta_{\mathrm{h}}-\beta_{\mathrm{f}}}{\beta_{\mathrm{f}}}C$ that does not vanish as $\eta \to \infty$. In a perfectly stealthy niche ($\beta_{\mathrm{f}} = \beta_{\mathrm{h}} = 1 > \beta_{\mathrm{v}} > 0$) the barrier disappears and B puts more mass on stealth than A exactly when $\eta(\Delta R + C) > \frac{\log(1/\beta_{\mathrm{v}})}{1-\beta_{\mathrm{v}}}$ — but in that niche the stealth-to-honest odds are the same under A and B, so whenever B has more stealth it also has more honesty and less cheating in total. B is worse than A on stealth there, and better on honesty and on total cheating.

**Three levers (sufficient conditions, not an exhaustive list).**
- **(R1) Prior barrier and maintenance cost** (exact condition for $\pi(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}) < \pi(\Pi_{\mathrm{h}})$ in this model, formalizing @testingthewaters): honesty dominates iff $\log(p_{\mathrm{h}}/p_{\mathrm{f}})$ exceeds the net exponent once capability/auxiliary costs $\delta_{\mathrm{maint}}$ are subtracted.
- **(R2) Audit decoupling — a condition on covariance, not on placement.** For a second, deployment monitor, under A the audit survival of iso-reward cheaters changes by exactly $\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_0}(\beta_{\mathrm{loop}}, \beta_{\mathrm{audit}})/\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[\beta_{\mathrm{loop}}]$: it does not increase iff that covariance is $\le 0$. Keeping the audit out of the loop does not by itself make the covariance non-positive (two detectors reading similar cues are positively correlated). Under B, non-positive covariance is necessary but not sufficient; the proved sufficient condition is independence or negative association.
- **(R3) Indistinguishable planted traps:** if traps are within total variation $\delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$ of ordinary episodes, any policy cheating at ordinary rate $\tau_{\mathrm{ord}} \ge \tau_{\min} > \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$ hits traps at rate $\ge \tau_{\mathrm{ord}} - \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$ and loses to honesty once $C$ exceeds an explicit threshold. Rare cheaters ($\tau_{\mathrm{ord}} < \tau_{\min}$, in particular $\le \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$) are not excluded.

**Sensitivity to how "discard" is modelled.** If rejection is applied at every round (iterated filtered fine-tuning), after $T$ rounds $\pi \propto \beta^T e^{T\eta_1 R}\pi_0$, and stealth eventually beats honesty iff $\eta_1\Delta R > \log(\beta_{\mathrm{h}}/\beta_{\mathrm{f}})$: the barrier no longer fades. So "pure rejection is fatal, penalty is not" depends on the modelling of the filter, and the honest summary is narrower than "a mathematical law".

The exact identities are classical (Price 1970; Robertson 1966; Fisher 1930), the Gibbs form of KL-regularized RL is known (Korbak, Perez & Buckley 2022), and "do not train against the chain-of-thought monitor" is already a published recommendation (Baker et al. 2025; Korbak et al. 2025).
