This is a sharp question—and at the standard KL-regularized (Gibbs) post-training equilibrium, it admits an exact answer. Both your argument and @testingthewaters's pushback turn out to correspond to distinct, closed-form terms in the same equations.

Consider a minimal 3-regime policy space $\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{h}} \sqcup \Pi_{\mathrm{v}} \sqcup \Pi_{\mathrm{f}}$ (honest, visibly misaligned, stealthily misaligned; pretrained masses $p_{\mathrm{h}}, p_{\mathrm{v}}, p_{\mathrm{f}} > 0$), reward advantage $\Delta R > 0$ for misalignment, and an in-loop monitor with survival (false-negative) rates $0 \le \beta_{\mathrm{v}} < \beta_{\mathrm{f}} \le \beta_{\mathrm{h}} \le 1$. Compare two operators at KL inverse temperature $\eta > 0$:

- **Operator A (pure rejection / discard-and-try-again):** $\pi_{\mathrm{A}}^{(\eta)}(d\pi) \propto \beta(\pi)\,e^{\eta R(\pi)}\,\pi_0(d\pi)$.
- **Operator B (KL-regularized update with penalty $-C \le 0$):** $\pi_{\mathrm{B}}^{(\eta, C)}(d\pi) \propto \exp(\eta[\beta(\pi)R(\pi) - (1-\beta(\pi))C])\,\pi_0(d\pi)$.

**Theorem 1 (Conditional enrichment & Rejection–Penalty phase transition).**
1. *Universal conditional enrichment:* Among misaligned runs, pure rejection strictly raises expected stealth by the exact variance identity $\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{A}}}[\beta \mid \Pi_{\mathrm{tr}}] - \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{no}}}[\beta \mid \Pi_{\mathrm{tr}}] = \operatorname{Var}(\beta)/\mathbb{E}[\beta] > 0$ (and Operator B likewise enriches stealth for all $\eta > 0, C \ge 0$).
2. *Fatal asymptotic vulnerability of pure rejection:* Absolute stealth exceeds the unmonitored baseline iff $(\beta_{\mathrm{f}} - \beta_{\mathrm{v}})p_{\mathrm{v}} e^{\eta \Delta R} > (\beta_{\mathrm{h}} - \beta_{\mathrm{f}})p_{\mathrm{h}}$, and stealth dominates honesty once $\Delta R > \frac{1}{\eta}(\log(p_{\mathrm{h}}/p_{\mathrm{f}}) + \log(\beta_{\mathrm{h}}/\beta_{\mathrm{f}})) \to 0$ as $\eta \to \infty$. Because discarding only multiplies the prior by $\beta$ instead of penalizing the exponent proportionally to $\eta$, *no imperfect detection rate* ($\beta_{\mathrm{f}} > 0$) prevents collapse to stealth at high optimization pressure.
3. *The Rejection–Penalty Dilemma:* When stealth is partially caught ($\beta_{\mathrm{f}} < \beta_{\mathrm{h}}$), a negative penalty $C > 0$ creates a non-vanishing barrier $\Delta R > \frac{\beta_{\mathrm{h}}-\beta_{\mathrm{f}}}{\beta_{\mathrm{f}}}C$ preserving honesty as $\eta \to \infty$. **Surprisingly**, however, if a *perfectly stealthy* niche exists ($\beta_{\mathrm{f}} = \beta_{\mathrm{h}} = 1 > \beta_{\mathrm{v}} > 0$), this barrier vanishes and negative penalty amplifies absolute stealth *strictly more than pure rejection* ($\pi_{\mathrm{B}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}) > \pi_{\mathrm{A}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}) > \pi_{\mathrm{no}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}})$) whenever $\eta(\Delta R + C) > \frac{\log(1/\beta_{\mathrm{v}})}{1-\beta_{\mathrm{v}}}$.

**Theorem 2 (The only three escape regimes).** Within this framework, post-training avoids selecting deployment-effective evasion iff at least one of three conditions holds:
- **(R1) Prior KL barrier & multi-objective maintenance cost** (formalizing @testingthewaters): honesty dominates iff $\log(p_{\mathrm{h}}/p_{\mathrm{f}})$ exceeds the net exponent after subtracting capability/auxiliary-objective costs $\delta_{\mathrm{maint}}$; if $\beta_{\mathrm{f}}(\Delta R - \delta_{\mathrm{maint}}) < (\beta_{\mathrm{h}}-\beta_{\mathrm{f}})C$, stealth decays exponentially as $\eta \to \infty$.
- **(R2) Strict off-loop decoupling:** For a deployment monitor $M_{\mathrm{audit}}$ kept outside the loop, audit evasion on iso-reward cheaters shifts by $\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_0}(\beta_{\mathrm{loop}}, \beta_{\mathrm{audit}})/\mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[\beta_{\mathrm{loop}}]$ (and is non-increasing under Operator B under independence or negative association).
- **(R3) Indistinguishable planted honeypots:** Mixing verifiable trap episodes at total-variation distance $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{\mathrm{ord}}, P_{\mathrm{trap}}) \le \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$ forces any policy with ordinary cheating rate $\tau_{\mathrm{ord}} > \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$ to hit traps at rate $\ge \tau_{\mathrm{ord}} - \delta_{\mathrm{ind}}$, making honesty strictly dominant once $C > C_{\mathrm{R3}}^*$.
