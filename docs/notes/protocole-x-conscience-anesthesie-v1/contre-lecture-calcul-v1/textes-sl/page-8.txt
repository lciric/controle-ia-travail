        3.1 Dynamical Regime Analysis

        According to the Critical Brain Hypothesis (Beggs & Plenz 2003, Tagliazucchi 2016),
        information transmission is maximized when the branching ratio $\sigma_l = |\Delta h_{l+1}| /
        |\Delta h_l|$ is poised at unity ($\sigma \approx 1$): - In the Awake Normal State, both
        models operate near criticality: GPT-2 ($\bar{\sigma} = 0.888$), Pythia-160M ($\bar{\sigma} =
        1.010$). - Under Deep Coma ($\alpha = 0.05$), perturbation propagation collapses, and
        downstream ratios diverge ($\bar{\sigma} > 1.45$), indicating sub-critical damping followed
        by unstable numerical amplification. - Inserting the Criticality Layer with homeostatic gain at
        Layer 5 pulls the branching ratio back toward criticality ($\bar{\sigma} = 1.241$ in GPT-2,
        $\bar{\sigma} = 1.276$ in Pythia), transiently boosting $PCI_{2D}$ from $0.3119$ to
        $0.3557$ in Pythia-160M.


        3.2 Epistemological Distinction

        The Criticality Layer must be understood as a thermodynamic/dynamical stabilizer, not an
        intentional reasoning engine. It re-establishes the physical medium necessary for information
        flow, but cannot restore the semantic weights eroded by structural lesioning.
