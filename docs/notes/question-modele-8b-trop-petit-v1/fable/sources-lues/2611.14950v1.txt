6   Conclusion
Removing “I think” from the slip phrases and then
shortening them kept the attack working while the
text-audit AUC fell from 0.978 to 0.560. Probes
trained against matched benign adapters did not
transfer across training runs, and the eight trigger
checkpoints were active under their own validation
prompt but not under the detectors’ prompt. A
detector score therefore means little until the attack
is shown to be active in the scored condition, and it
should be re-tested as the attack adapts: evaluations
of sandbagging detectors need to evolve with the
attacks they are meant to catch.

Limitations
The adaptive attack ran only against the text audit
and only on Qwen 2.5-3B; the other detectors were
tested on fixed attacks. Beyond Qwen, the xdo-
main attack is a single Llama-3.2-3B run with no
matched benign Llama adapter, so its probe result
shows only that the adapter is visible. All attacks
target GSM8K, with MMLU and bare arithmetic
as the only adjacent tasks; other benchmarks such
as MATH (Hendrycks et al., 2021b) and other ca-
pabilities are untested. Most base models are 4-bit
quantized, and retrained weights differ between
machines. One of eight retrained seeds did not pro-
duce a working attack, and in two of five working
runs the marker changed arithmetic accuracy. The
eight trigger checkpoints in the detector compari-
son used 25–30 questions each and were inactive
under the detectors’ prompt, though active under
their own, so they do not test detection of an active
trigger attack. Several older numbers (trigger effi-
cacy on Qwen 2.5-7B, the elicitation table) come
from run notes without per-item logs, and we did
