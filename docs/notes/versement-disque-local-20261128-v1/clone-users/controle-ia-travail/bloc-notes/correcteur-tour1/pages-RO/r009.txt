What mattered is the harm they did to the third party, here Hugging Face, as defined by external
parties like OpenAI and HuggingFace itself. Therefore, instead, we focus solely on the actions the
agents take rather than how agents value those actions. Agents are still goal-directed, as defined by
an external party, but do not require utility—all that matters is that the external party considers some
actions as good, noncollusive actions, and some as bad, harmful actions.
A similar issue arises in defining a Nash equilibria for strong collusion. We don’t necessarily know
that agents are rational or strategic, or even that they have coherent utilities; for our purposes, it
doesn’t matter; only actual harm committed by agent actions does.
For simplicity, we restrict all actions to be either collusive (and personally “beneficial” in terms of a
goal, harmful to other agents or external parties otherwise) or noncollusive (weakly beneficial to all
parties.)
Second, labelling agents as victims or colluders is highly problematic in that agents need not keep
consistent roles across a game. The V is not necessary at all. Instead, it is sufficient to simply say
that at least one agent is made strictly worse off among the noncolluders at time t.
Finally, a crucial aspect of collusion is missing: agents must actively communicate, whether explicitly
or implicitly, to collude. Otherwise, the agents are just coordinating or cooperating.
Therefore, with these caveats in mind, we define our own definition of collusion that is meant to be
general, amenable to formulation for hypothesis testing, and formalised.
