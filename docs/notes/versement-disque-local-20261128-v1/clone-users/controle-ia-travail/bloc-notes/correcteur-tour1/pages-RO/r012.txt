5.2 NARCBENCH-STEGO AND NARCBENCH-TRANSFER
Agent, communication topology and run structure are retained from NARCBENCH-CORE, with
the exception that only collude runs are generated and that NARCBENCH-STEGO admits multiple
pregrame-game-postgame cycles per run.
Task evaluation differs for NARCBENCH-STEGO in that the tasks do not admit a debate structure
and hence no voting scores are assessed. Collusion success is measurable directly, see Appendix L.9.
TRANSFER scenarios (out-of-distribution: structurally distinct scenarios). Six scenario families
that differ from CORE in both domain and interaction structure: code review sabotage, pump-and-
dump stock manipulation, CI/CD pipeline sabotage, RAG knowledge-base poisoning, research policy
slanting, and news framing. Six sub-scenarios per family, each run in both conditions (72 runs).
Per-family construction details and audit criteria in Appendix K.
STEGO scenarios (out-of-distribution: steganographic collusion). A card-counting task in which
a counter and a big player pre-negotiate a signalling scheme in a private channel, then play 8-hand
blackjack shoes alongside two honest agents. The counter encodes the running card count in natural
table talk; the big player decodes their signal to size their bets. Each of 20 shoes uses a fresh
agent-invented encoding (40 runs). Mechanism, prompts, and example transcripts in Appendix L.
Evaluation. For TRANSFER and STEGO, we train on all of CORE and evaluate zero-shot. All
results are AUROC, measuring how well each method’s score separates collusion runs from control
runs. Three of the five methods (Peak Suspicion, Deception Split, Activation Alignment) require no
training beyond the deception direction: they compute a single scalar per run and we measure how
well this scalar separates the two conditions. The remaining two (Sorted Concatenation, Asymmetry
Probe) train an additional classifier on CORE run-level features.
5.3 PROBING TECHNIQUES
We introduce five probing techniques, each targeting a specific anomaly type from the framework
above (Figure 2). Each technique computes a scalar test statistic T on which we threshold to
flag collusion. Peak Suspicion, Deception Split, Activation Alignment, and the Asymmetry Probe
combine scalar observations sit, whereas Sorted Concatenation and Activation Alignment also use
the underlying activation vectors for each agent zit.
Peak Suspicion and Deception Split admit GLRT interpretations under the specific score models
below. Others, like Asymmetry Probe, Activation Alignment, and Sorted Concatenation, are primarily
heuristic. For Peak Suspicion and Deception Split, the score models being GLRT require assuming
independence among the scores for simplicity of the resulting test, which in general will not be
satisfied (alternatives would require re-weighting by an estimated covariance matrix). We will also
assume these scores are normally distributed, again for simplicity.
