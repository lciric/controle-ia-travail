Various conceptual arguments have been brought
forward concerning the selection of optimal probe
architectures: Hewitt & Liang (2019) argue that non-
linear probes might learn the probing task itself
rather than read out model representations, and the
linear representation hypothesis (Park et al., 2024)
proposes that the internal representations of many
properties of concern may be inherently linearly sep-
arable. In contrast, Pimentel et al. (2020) argue for
non-linear probes based on an information-theoretic
argument, and the empirical literature contains fur-
ther evidence in favour of non-linear probe perfor-
mance (McKenzie et al., 2025; Oldfield et al., 2026). We consider both linear and non-linear probe
architectures (see Appendix E), and note that probe architecture selection is ultimately a model
selection problem adjudicated by empirical generalisation performance (Larson, 1931; Stone, 1974).
