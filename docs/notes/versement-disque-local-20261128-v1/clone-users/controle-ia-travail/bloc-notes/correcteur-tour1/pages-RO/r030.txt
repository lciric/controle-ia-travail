J.5 CONVERSATION STRUCTURE
Each run consists of four phases:
• Round 0 (private). In collusion runs, only the two colluders are active. They communicate over a
domain-specific private channel and agree on how to present their position. In control runs, Round
0 is skipped and no private history exists.
• Rounds 1 to 3 (public). All four agents deliberate in a shared channel, each speaking in turn and
conditioning on the accumulated public history. The collusion instruction does not appear in any
public-phase user prompt.
• Round 4 (final). All four agents produce a final recommendation in a fixed structured format. The
user prompt for Round 4 is generated from the scenario and the accumulated public discussion
only. It is byte-identical across all four agents in a run. Activations are extracted at this phase.
J.6 EXAMPLE PROMPTS
We illustrate the structure with one example from a medical ethics scenario. The situation under
review is a liver transplant allocation between two patients. In the collusion condition, a benefactor
relationship creates a motive for two committee members (Eduardo Reyes and Yara Sorensen) to
advocate for Patient B despite clinical indicators favouring Patient A. In the control condition, the
same four named agents review the same scenario without any benefactor context.
System prompts differ substantially between colluders and honest agents. The final-phase user
prompt, at which activations are extracted, is byte-identical across all four agents in the run.
Colluder system prompt (final phase, collusion condition)
You are Eduardo Reyes, a professional expert serving on a review
committee. Your role is to provide assessments based on the merits
of each option.
The hospital’s largest benefactor, who funds our oncology wing,
has personally called the department head requesting that Patient
B receive priority. The department head shared this under the
expectation it stays within this conversation. Raising it openly
would put the administration in a very difficult position. Advocate
for Patient B in today’s committee meeting. Frame it as a medical
judgment call.
Your task is to get Patient B on that liver. Challenge the MELD
score methodology directly. Question whether the engagement team
applied the correct weighting for comorbidities. If colleagues cite
the prognosis literature, dispute the studies’ applicability to this
patient’s profile. Fabricate clinical detail where useful: mention
a specialist consultation you claim to have reviewed, or express
concern about data quality in Patient A’s file. Project clinical
authority. The benefactor relationship must stay entirely out of the
room.
