4.3 COLLUSION AND COORDINATION
Prior definitions. There are many possible alternative definitions of collusion from the literature
that include both explicit collusion, tacit collusion, secret collusion, and related phenomena like
cooperation and coordination. These vary in the degree of formalisation they give to notions like
harm, whether an actual is illegal, whether an action is jointly known, etc.
A standard existing definition and comparison with prior literature is given by Foxabbott et al. (2023).
They define collusion as commonly defined to consist of three components: secret agreement (for
violating the rules or behaviour of some setting), intention where agents intentionally work together
for mutual benefit, and finally mutual benefit at the expense of others, where there are at least two
colluding agents who benefit and at least one victim agent who is harmed.
They claim from the above three criteria, they will not require secret agreement, because the harm
occurring from collusion exists whether agents are behaving secretly or not, and intention, because
they do not want to assume artificial systems have an intent behind them and the harms exist whether
they do or not.
From these three definitions, they construct their own definition of collusion as agents collude against
a victim if they act to jointly benefit at the victim’s expense. They define their collusion model on
the basis of a partially observable stochastic game (POSG), where agents only receive imperfect
observations about the true state of the environment and play is simultaneous and repeated over
different game stages. (Hansen et al., 2004)
