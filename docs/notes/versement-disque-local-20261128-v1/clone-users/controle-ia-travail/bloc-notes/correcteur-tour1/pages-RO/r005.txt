level-α test. Unlike the guarantees provided by the Neyman-Pearson lemma for simple hypothesis
testing, a UMP test need not exist for a composite alternative and its existence generally depends on
the specified distributions and parameter space.
In our setting, the general problem of detecting an unknown subset of agents, no UMP has been
shown to exist to the authors’ knowledge. Indeed it seems likely that it does not exist. This is
because the “optimal” test, if it exists, would likely depend on signal density (how many groups G
are actually likely to collude) and strength (how distinguishable samples from binary outcomes are).
See Addario-Berry et al. (2010) for further related discussion for a fixed size-k group setting.
For general composite alternatives, a natural test is the generalised likelihood-ratio test (GLRT).
Here, X is once again a sample of data and X is drawn from some PG(· | θ1) if the hypothesis group
G colludes is true and X ∼ P0(·|θ0) if no group colludes. The test rejects H0 when ΛGLR(X) ≥ η,
with a constant η chosen that the rejection is α at most under every distribution in the family of
distributions possible under the null hypothesis, and fails to reject H0 otherwise.
