3.3 REPRESENTATION PROBING
Representation probes (Alain & Bengio, 2018) are classifiers that act on activations, which are
intermediate computation states in model forward passes. In the case of transformer architectures,
activations are commonly extracted from the residual stream (Elhage et al., 2021) at a fixed layer
and token position. Probes are trained to predict target properties of the model or model input,
such as truthfulness (Azaria & Mitchell, 2023; Burns et al., 2023; Marks & Tegmark, 2024) or
deception (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025). See Appendix Q for an in-depth discussion of probe
architecture choice.
4 HELIOS: A FRAMEWORK FOR MULTI-AGENT COLLUSION DETECTION
4.1 ENVIRONMENT
We now define a theoretical framework for multi-agent collusion detection. We consider a modifica-
tion to a setting called a n-player general-sum Finite-Horizon Sequential game as defined in Sokota
et al. (2023). It describes settings in which players act or communicate one-at-a-time each round
(unlike a standard POSG) and in which the game terminates after a fixed number of steps T. Kovařík
et al. (2022) establish an earlier form of this setting and show it is equivalent to a subset of the more
standard imperfect information extensive form games called perfect-recall timeable Extensive Form
Game (Jakobsen et al., 2016). We use this setting because it clearly distinguishes observations that
are common knowledge and those that are private and because it is amenable to our setting.
Our primary modification will be that while the Finite-Horizon Sequential game features known
rewards, we will not assume any rewards are present. Subsequently, we will also describe an added
monitoring process. We will call this a Finite-Horizon Sequential AI Game.
