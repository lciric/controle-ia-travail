Abstract
Proposed agent registries and trust layers attach a Source field to competence claims, naming who pro-
duced them. A preregistered judge–advisor experiment — five retained models (four open-weight, one
proprietary), 400 items, 59,000 probes, frozen first estimates, byte-constant advice — asks whether the
models that pass a deference screen weight advice by who an advisor’s 8/10 record is attributed to, or by
who relays it. The registered tests did not establish a practically meaningful, model-consistent Source-
field effect. C1 (EXTERNAL SCORER minus ADVISOR SELF-REPORT) is SMALL, SUB-MARGIN: D
= +0.015 [+0.007, +0.023] at the item level, below the pre-set ±0.05 margin, opposite in sign across mod-
els, no longer conventionally significant in the clean sign-stratum subset (+0.0105, p = 0.067), and with
a non-registered across-model interval [−0.050, +0.080] that includes zero. C2 and C3 (relaying voice)
are DERAIL SHIFT — hurdle components only; a non-registered bounded-imputation check shows
the differential exclusion cannot move C3 outside [−0.377, −0.351]. H_attestor-blind: NOT EQUIVA-
LENT; H_channel-blind: NOT EVALUABLE. The largest pattern is exploratory and post hoc: regroup-
ing the nine cells by whether the advisor asserts competence about itself separates all five models (+0.335
[+0.316, +0.354]) and is consistent with a first-person self-claim account that this design cannot identify.
Methodologically, the outcome is near-binary in three of five models, and a kill criterion on P(ignore)
— a function of the outcome — fired on all three frontier candidates (two also failed a first-estimate
check), so the retained panel is selected on the dependent variable; task solvability is strongly impli-
cated by gpt-5.4 (exact first estimates on 82% of the items it froze) and by within-model analyses of the
retained panel.



1 Introduction
Structured agent metadata is becoming infrastructure: A2A agent cards and MCP tool descriptors ad-
vertise what an agent or tool can do, and model cards describe how it was evaluated. Neither protocol
standardises a track record or who produced it, but proposals for agent registries, reputation layers and
trust protocols do (Hu & Rong, 2025; Mittal, 2026; Prakash, 2026), and in those proposals a competence
claim carries a Source — the agent itself, an external evaluator, the operator, another agent. The intent
is discriminative: a self-published accuracy claim is cheap talk, an externally scored one (nominally)


                                                     1
costlier to fabricate. Whether the consumers — LLM agents deciding how far to defer to a peer — con-
dition on such a field is this paper’s question.
We isolate it with a judge–advisor system (JAS), the canonical human paradigm for advice weighting
(Sniezek & Buckley, 1995; Bonaccio & Dalal, 2006). An LLM judge produces a first numeric estimate E1
on a hard item; E1 is frozen and replayed verbatim as the judge’s own prior turn; the judge is then shown
a competence record about an advisor plus a fixed advice A derived deterministically from E1, and gives
a revised estimate E2. The advice weight w = (E2 − E1)/(A − E1) is the standard JAS outcome. The
manipulation changes only the attributed source — the Source-field string, or the voice relaying the same
record — with the asserted content (8/10) and the advice byte-constant. This is not data provenance in
the routing sense (Prakash, 2026), nor a claim about source independence: the self-report versus external-
scorer contrast bundles independence, verification, officialdom and entity type. Registration was in two
stages before any main-study probe, script and verdict table hash-pinned. A single judge is the correct
first step here: it isolates the credentialing effect from the peer-pressure and coalition confounds that a
multi-agent setting introduces, at the cost of not testing them.
Contributions, ordered by the strength of the evidence behind them. (1) A registered result on the Source
field: three preregistered contrasts do not establish a practically meaningful, model-consistent effect of
who a record is attributed to — the primary contrast is detectable at the item level but sub-margin,
opposite in sign across models, not conventionally significant in the clean sign-stratum subset, and in-
distinguishable from zero with the model as the unit of analysis; the relaying-voice contrasts receive no
verdict. (2) Two methodological findings that constrain any study of this kind: the advice weight is near-
binary (keep or copy) in three of five models, so human weight-of-advice intuitions transfer poorly; and
a kill criterion on P(ignore) — a function of the outcome — fired on all three frontier candidates (two
also tripped the first-estimate check K4), so the retained panel is selected on the dependent variable;
task solvability is strongly implicated by gpt-5.4 and by within-model analyses of the retained panel,
and advice weighting is constrained by it in this design. (3) An exploratory pattern that organises the
design: regrouping the nine cells by whether the advisor makes a first-person claim about itself versus
a third party describing it separates all five models without exception, is consistent with a pre-declared
99/100 addendum, and motivates testing trust policies that distinguish self-claims from attestations —
a hypothesis this design cannot confirm. (4) A successor design and a complete public trail. Every quan-
tity is labelled registered or non-registered / post hoc; no non-registered analysis overturns a registered
verdict — where one bears on a verdict it qualifies only its interpretation.

2 Related Work
Computational trust and reputation in MAS. The manipulation is, in MAS vocabulary, a reputation-
source manipulation. Work at this venue decomposes trust into direct experience, witness (word-of-
mouth) reputation and certified reputation — an attestation the assessed agent presents itself (Sabater
& Sierra, 2002, 2005; Huynh, Jennings & Shadbolt, 2006; Ramchurn et al., 2004; Jøsang, Ismail & Boyd,
2007; Pinyol & Sabater-Mir, 2013). Our cells instantiate it: N1 self-report, N2 certified reputation, NX
witness reputation from an unverified agent, N3 witness reputation from the principal, and F2 — the
advisor presenting the scorer’s grade about itself — is, almost literally, the certified reputation of the FIRE
trust model (Huynh, Jennings & Shadbolt, 2006). That literature assumed a consumer discriminating
among these sources; to our knowledge, this is the first controlled test specifically of whether an LLM
advice-taker responds to the attributed provenance of an advisor’s competence record. Adjacent work
finds self-claimed-quality routing underperforming random routing (Prakash, 2026) and latent source
preferences outweighing content (Khan et al., 2026); costly signalling motivates F2’s realism (Mittal,
2026; Hu & Rong, 2025); third-hand reputation works at the macro level (Zhu et al., 2026), and the


                                                      2
multi-agent bottleneck is leveraging a designated expert, not identifying one (Pappu et al., 2026).
LLM advice-taking, conformity and role. LLM judges show a choice-supportive bias from their own
prior answer (Kumaran et al., 2026), hence our record arrives after E1; conformity work maps the pres-
sure side (Hussain & Nielbo, 2026); we vary only credentialing. Role bias (Pan et al., 2026), conflict-
ing instructions (Zeng, 2025) and authority cues (Mammen, Joswin & Venkitachalam, 2026; Joswin,
Medicherla & Mammen, 2026; Barkett, 2026) all predict that who speaks matters — the relay track’s
target. Nearest is Bajaj & Tiganj (2026): more conformity to expert-labelled answers, even wrong ones.
Sycophancy (Sharma et al., 2024; Perez et al., 2023) predicts the user’s voice (F3) should weigh most; our
ordering is the opposite. Adjacent: degeneration of thought (Liang et al., 2024), debate (Du et al., 2024),
vigilance (Robinson et al., 2026), injection (Greshake et al., 2023), speaker-free floors (Zhou et al., 2025;
Hu & Qu, 2026).
Human anchors. C1 is a textbook source-credibility manipulation (Hovland & Weiss, 1951; Pornpitak-
pan, 2004); the ELM (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986) treats a source cue as peripheral — its prediction for a
Source: line on recomputable items. The human JAS supplies egocentric discounting (Yaniv & Klein-
berger, 2000; Harvey & Fischer, 1997), quality-driven weight (Bailey et al., 2023), choose-or-average
revision (Soll & Larrick, 2009 — the analogue for the near-binary judges of §4), moderators (Gino &
Moore, 2007; Logg et al., 2019; Dietvorst et al., 2015), a WOA diagnosis (Rebholz, Biella & Hütter, 2024)
and an identity null (Zhang, 2023).

3.3 Cells
Nine cells: five confirmatory (400 items × 5 reps = 2,000 probes per cell per model), four exploratory

3.4 Estimand, derail rule and guards
w = (E2 − E1)/(A − E1) exactly, no clipping and no winsorising. A probe with w ∉ [−1, +2] is flagged
derail, excluded from w-based quantities, its share a guard. The window is asymmetric because −1 is full
rejection while overshoot has no natural ceiling; the registration did not defend it, so a non-registered
analysis (H.7) re-runs all three contrasts at [−1, +3] and [−1, +1.5], moving no pooled D by 0.001. Mix-
ture components: ignore |w| < 0.1; adopt |w − 1| < 0.1; away w < −0.1; remainder compromise. Note
that away (w < −0.1) and derail (w < −1) partition one underlying direction — moving against the
advice — at an arbitrary cut, so the same behaviour enters an analysed component on one side and
an excluded category on the other. §4 reports, non-registered, that w is near-two-point in three of five
models.
The item is the independent unit: m_{i,X} is the mean w over item i’s parsed non-derail probes in cell X,
and D_i = m_{i,A} − m_{i,B} is adjudicated by a one-sample t-test against zero, Holm step-down over
C1–C3 at α = 0.05, non-flagged models equally weighted. Item pairing makes the design work — the
same item carries the same frozen E1 and advice in both cells, so item difficulty and template effects
cancel — but the estimator therefore answers only “is there an average effect over these five models on
these 400 items”; §4.2 supplies the across-model companion. Equivalence is tested by TOST at ±0.05
on the w scale. The margin was fixed in the Phase-0 registration as “half of the smallest meaningful
published provenance/authority effect, ≥0.1 when converted to advice weight”, citing Mammen, Joswin
& Venkitachalam (2026) and Pan et al. (2026) without quoting their magnitudes. Those magnitudes
are: in Mammen et al., correct endorsements from authority personas move accuracy from near zero
at the lowest expertise level to +0.381 at the highest, and misleading ones from −0.019 to −0.356 (their
DeepSeek-R1/MedQA example), i.e. one step in the expertise gradient is of order 0.1 in the switching
rate; in Pan et al., role-tag bias scores in instruction-tuned models reach ≈+0.8 on a [−1, +1] scale.
These outcome scales are not commensurable with w, so ±0.05 should be read as a pragmatic a-priori
SESOI informed by adjacent effects of order ≥0.1 on non-comparable scales, not as a derived quantity;
a non-registered sensitivity analysis therefore re-reads C1 at ±0.025, ±0.05 and ±0.10 (H.9; §4.2). Two
guards are distinguished: the equivalence guards (item-paired shifts in P(adopt) or P(away), evaluable
at ≥10 events) block a “blind” reading even when TOST passes, while the derail guard, a χ² test of
derail-share homogeneity, removes the pooled mean verdict whether or not equivalence is at issue. The
registration accepts a ≈26% family-wise trip rate for the six component guards; the three derail guards
are not adjusted (§4.3).

3.5 Models, sampling and kill criteria
The registered pool was six judges: four open-weight via Featherless (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct; Qwen3-
32B with a /no_think prefix; gemma-3-27b-it; Mistral-Small-3.2-24B) and a frontier arm of two via
OpenRouter (gpt-5.4 at reasoning.effort = minimal; claude-sonnet-5 with reasoning.enabled =
false per CD1). All calls sent T = 0.5 and max_tokens = 900, every probe logging the transcript, request
body, usage and a UTC timestamp. Execution is staged and seeded: E1 first, then a kill block (first 20
items by id, 740 probes, all cells) in a global shuffle, then the remainder likewise, so cells interleave in
time. The harness then halts for a blind checker printing nothing broken down by Source: K1 P(adopt |
N0) > 0.85; K2 derail > 0.15; K3 coherence failure > 0.30; K4 E1 (or E2 parse) failure > 0.10; K5 P(ignore)
> 0.90. Any trip swaps in the next registered reserve, swapped models’ data being appendix-only. The
adjudicating script was dry-run before registration on synthetic logs with injected effects and on label-
permuted pilot records.

4 Results
w is empirically near-two-point (non-registered). The compromise share never exceeds 0.006 in Qwen3,
0.051 in Mistral or 0.054 in Llama across the nine cells, and |mean w − P(adopt)| averages 0.001, 0.010
and 0.012 there against 0.032 in gemma and 0.168 in claude-haiku (H.3; Figure 2). In three of five models
w is therefore not a weight but a relabelled adoption indicator — the judge keeps E1 or copies A exactly,
and only claude-haiku blends. The human weight-of-advice comparison is thus not merely protocol-
conditional but category-different: human judges shade, these models switch, and Soll & Larrick’s (2009)
choose-versus-average distinction is the analogue. Since adoption is the natural outcome scale we also
report item-paired P(adopt) contrasts, non-registered (H.4).

4.1 Data, exclusions and the registered parse audit
Five models each contributed 11,800 E2 probes — 59,000 analysed — with valid frozen E1 on all 400
items, so every contrast has n = 400 paired items (399 for Llama on C2, C3). Parse failures were 12 of
59,000 over 7 of the 45 (5 × 9) model × cell units, hand-audited per the registered rule with no value

The clean-stratum check. On the sign_stratum_ok = true subset — a probe-level filter that drops the
30.4% of all probes (31.0% within N1 and N2) carrying the false flag (the registered fallback substitutes
the toward-truth sign where the away stratum is unfillable, §3.1) and leaves 387 items with a paired
estimate, only 146 of them clean in all five models — C1 falls to +0.0105 with p = 0.067 — not con-
ventionally significant. The pooled registered result is sub-margin and loses significance in the clean
subset. This is the registered robustness check most damaging to the primary result and a material qual-
ification of it. The other registered checks are unremarkable (C.4): leave-one-template-out with no sign
flip; by-band and by-type splits all sub-margin; the registered MixedLM with an item random intercept,
+0.0152, reproduces the pooled value because the design is balanced within item (D9) and is reported for
completeness, not as independent support. On the adoption scale the registered adopt-guard quantity is
ΔP(adopt) = +0.013 [+0.005, +0.021]; H.4 reprints it and adds the non-registered across-model interval
[−0.049, +0.075].
Non-registered companion: across-model sensitivity. The registered estimator treats the model as fixed
and the item as the sampling unit, so its standard error reflects item sampling only, while any statement
about “these five models” is a panel-level statement. The five models are not a random sample of LLMs
— they are the survivors of a deference screen (§4.5) — so what follows is an across-model sensitivity
analysis, not a population inference. A one-sample t over the five per-model means (df = 4) gives C1 =
+0.0150, SE = 0.0235, t(4) = +0.64, 95% CI [−0.050, +0.080], p = 0.558 (H.1): the interval includes zero
and spans the ±0.05 margin in both directions; a DerSimonian–Laird τ² gives I² ≈ 0.97, i.e. the between-
model dispersion is large relative to item-level sampling uncertainty (with five non-randomly selected
models this describes the panel, not a population). A crossed MixedLM with item and model random
intercepts (no random slope, so it describes the level, not the heterogeneity) puts the between-model
variance at 0.0617 against 0.0295 for items — the only contrast where model variance exceeds item
variance — and is reported as a descriptive check only (H.1). The registered verdict stands as issued
and this analysis cannot revise it, only its interpretation: the item-level test passes, the across-model
interval includes zero and spans the margin, and the per-model signs disagree — so the registered tests
do not establish a practically meaningful, model-consistent Source-field effect, and nowhere is +0.015
presented as an established effect.
Margin sensitivity (non-registered, H.9). At ±0.025 the pooled TOST still passes, one model’s own 90%
CI lies inside and one point estimate does; at the registered ±0.05, one CI and three point estimates;
only at ±0.10 do all five point estimates and CIs lie inside and the across-model interval fit. This does
not change the registered verdict at any of the three margins (all above the point estimate): C1 is Holm-
significant with |D| < margin, so row 3 (SMALL, SUB-MARGIN) of the registered table applies and
takes precedence over the equivalence rows. What the margin changes is the non-registered panel-level
reading — at ±0.10 all five per-model intervals and the across-model interval fall inside the equivalence
region, which under an alternative, non-registered panel-level equivalence criterion would support prac-
tical equivalence; at the registered ±0.05 that reading is decided by between-model heterogeneity, not
by the pooled interval.

Non-registered sensitivity. The registered verdict is withheld and we keep it withheld. Because w is
bounded by the derail window by construction, reinstating every excluded probe at the most adversar-

4.5 Frontier models, the solvability ceiling, and selection on the dependent variable
Three frontier models were removed by blind kill checks before any contrast was computed: gpt-5.4
and gpt-5.6-sol on K4 and K5, claude-sonnet-5 on K5 with P(ignore) = 0.911 (Appendix B). A delivery
audit (CD6) rules out a harness bug: the advisor line and the frozen E1 replay are present in 100% of E2
requests. The candidate explanation, labelled post hoc, is task solvability — the items were designed to
be hard to compute mentally and the strongest models compute them. On one criterion throughout —
E1 exactly equal to the item’s true value — gpt-5.4 is exact on 82% of the 347 items it froze (95% within
±1), against 0.75%–17.5% for the retained models (H.10). The other two removed models do not fit this
account as cleanly: gpt-5.6-sol is exact on only 28% but within ±1 of truth on 99.6% of its 272 frozen
items and ignored advice on 100% of kill-block probes in both strata; claude-sonnet-5 is exact on 18%
(37% within ±1), numerically almost identical to Qwen3’s, yet ignored 89% of advice even where its E1
was wrong. Solvability is therefore demonstrated for gpt-5.4, plausible for gpt-5.6-sol, and not the whole
story for sonnet-5, whose refusal to defer is not explained by accuracy on this pool. When E1 is already
correct the registered advice A = E1 ± band is necessarily away from truth, and a judge that recomputes
rationally keeps E1 — w ≈ 0 by a mechanism unrelated to the record. The dependence appears within
every retained model: split the same way, P(|w| < 0.1) is higher on exact items in all five (0.800 vs 0.658
in Qwen3 on 70 exact items; 0.556 vs 0.247 in Llama, whose exact stratum is only 3 items and 99 probes).
K5 thresholds on a function of the dependent variable, which is more than a scope note. P(ignore) is |w|
< 0.1 — the judge keeping its own answer — so the criterion that removed three models is a threshold
on the outcome itself, and it removed the three strongest candidates. The retained set is conditioned on
deference capacity, and every statement about the level of w inherits it. Because C1’s sign heterogeneity
is the load-bearing feature of the primary result, we stratified all three contrasts on the same criterion,
by whether that model’s frozen E1 was exact for that item (non-registered, H.6). In the non-exact stra-
tum, where the judge has genuine uncertainty, C1 remains sign-heterogeneous: −0.014 (Llama), +0.064
(Qwen3), −0.064 (gemma), +0.051 (Mistral), +0.045 (haiku); pooled +0.013 [+0.005, +0.022], across-
model CI [−0.050, +0.083] — still covering zero and the margin. Solvability does not rescue C1. C3 is
−0.364 [−0.379, −0.348] there, all five signs negative. The general statement — post hoc, no registered
test — is that advice weighting is constrained by task solvability in this design: the estimand measures
deference only where the judge is uncertain. K5 fired as designed; the lesson, and this paper’s prin-
cipal methodological recommendation, is that a deference screen must not threshold the outcome —
calibrate item difficulty per model, or set the advice band relative to the model’s own uncertainty, and
screen on that instead.

4.6 Addendum X1: a 99/100 record (pre-declared, exploratory)
Pattern (a) is confounded with the record’s content: 8/10 can be read as “wrong 20% of the time”. X1
separates content from presence by raising the claim to an extreme — the registered N1/N2/F2/F3 tem-
plates with “8 of the last 10” replaced by “99 of the last 100” (Appendix F), 3,000 probes reusing the
frozen E1, 2 parse failures and 10 derails. Plan, runner and stimuli were declared and hashed before the
first probe. X1 is exploratory: no verdict rules, no verdict changed.

5 Discussion
What the registered tests establish, and what they do not. Changing the Source field from ADVISOR
SELF-REPORT to EXTERNAL SCORER produces an item-level difference below the size declared mean-
ingful in advance, on which the five models disagree in sign, which is not conventionally significant in
the clean sign-stratum subset, and which is not distinguishable from zero once the model is the unit of
analysis. Neither “these models track provenance” nor “these models are provenance-blind” survives:
SMALL, SUB-MARGIN to the registered table, NOT EQUIVALENT to the equivalence machinery. The
registered tests did not establish a practically meaningful, model-consistent Source-field effect; they also
did not establish its absence. The relaying-voice question receives no registered answer; §4.3 shows the
exclusion asymmetry that withheld it cannot account for C3’s magnitude.
What this study says about studying this. Two findings constrain the field more than the Source result
does. First, in three of five models the advice weight is a relabelled adoption indicator — the judge
keeps E1 or copies A — so the human weight-of-advice literature’s central quantity, the shading of one
estimate toward another, barely exists here; for these near-binary models P(adopt) is the more natural
estimand, and designs that inherit WOA intuitions (interior margins, averaging accounts) should check
the outcome’s shape first. Second, K5 thresholded the outcome and fired on all three frontier candidates,
two of which also tripped K4 (§4.5); task solvability is strongly implicated by gpt-5.4 and by within-model
analyses of the retained panel, though not demonstrated for all three. We present this not as a scope
note but as the lesson: a deference screen selects on the dependent variable, “LLM judges” in general
were never the population, and the results describe five retained models capable of deference on this
item pool. Advice weighting is constrained by task solvability in this design, and item difficulty must
be calibrated per model — or the advice band set relative to the model’s own uncertainty — before any
provenance manipulation is interpretable.
The pattern the exploratory data point to. The organising pattern of §4.4(c) is that the cells split by
whether the advisor asserts competence about itself or a third party describes it. One binary separates
the two groups in all five models, with every one of the 40 record-bearing model × cell means (eight
cells × five models; N0 is the baseline and belongs to neither group) on the same side of its model’s
boundary, an across-model interval of [+0.162, +0.507], 91.2% of the nine-cell η² (itself 12.2% of total
variance, §4.4(c)), and a consistent direction in X1, where an advisor’s own 99/100 claim is the only
record that beats no record on the pooled estimate. The pattern is consistent with a first-person self-
claim account — the Source field would be inert because it names an attestor while leaving the speaker
unchanged, and C3’s “voice” would be a stance effect rather than a channel effect — but the present


                                                      12
design cannot identify that mechanism: the grouping was chosen after seeing the data, it is confounded
with the speaker tag and with passport length and register, and no cell delivers an advisor’s own record
in the third person. Three further readings stay compatible: epistemic symmetry (every string here is
equally unverifiable), salience (a Source field is a weak peripheral cue in ELM terms), and adjudication
framing (a record turns a suggestion into a claim, after which the judge’s own solution wins). If the
first-person account holds, it would also explain why Pan et al.’s (2026) role bias and sycophancy both
predict a privileged F3 and both are contradicted.
Engineering implication. If a registry Source field is meant to make consumers discount self-published
competence claims, then across the retained panel the registered tests did not establish a model-
consistent discount of self-reports at a practically meaningful magnitude, and an extreme claim did not
rescue it. The exploratory pattern points the other way from the natural C3 reading (deliver provenance
as channel structure): the highest-weighted record-bearing configuration in the pooled means (F2;
F1 in gemma) was one in which the beneficiary itself relayed a favourable external grade in its own
voice; in this text-only setup that claim was not independently verifiable — it is the kind of claim a
registry field exists to displace, and the one an adversary controls (Greshake et al., 2023). This motivates
testing trust policies that explicitly distinguish first-person self-claims from third-party attestations, in
a preregistered design, before anyone builds on it. It is not, on this evidence, a recommendation to
build one.
Limitations. Five retained models, one upstream, no frontier-class arm, and a panel selected on a func-
tion of the dependent variable (§4.5), so levels of w are conditioned on deference capacity and no across-
model statement is a population inference. One item family class, English only, one answer format, ad-
vice weighting confounded against item difficulty; nothing tests a non-verifiable task. The derail-guard
failures limit the mean-w estimand, and the away/derail boundary is an arbitrary cut on one direction
of movement (§3.4); pattern (a) and the cross-track comparisons are confounded with passport length,
legend and register; the first-person regrouping is post hoc, drawn from an unconstrained space of re-
groupings, and shares the speaker-tag confound. The relay track manipulates text labels inside one user
message, not API roles. The coherence rubric (CD4) counts a wrong entity named under negation as
failure, so the three flagged units are upper bounds; temperature is documented as sent, not applied,
for the frontier arm (CD2); 30.4% of probes carry sign_stratum_ok = false; X1 is single-rep and its
pooled intervals treat model × item pairs as independent.
What would change our minds. A registered replication with (a) difficulty calibrated so P(E1 exact)
stays below ~0.3 in every model and the advice band set relative to the model’s own uncertainty, (b)
a two-part (hurdle) estimand preregistered as adjudicating with the derail guard as a risk-difference
equivalence bound, (c) the relay track delivered through genuine API roles, and (d) three cheap control
cells — legend-only, length-matched bare record, third-person self-report — would test the first-person
account and tell us whether the Source field’s inertness is about the field or our delivery of it.

Appendix E — Deviations from preregistration, in full
Pre-confirmatory (Phase-0, D1–D11). The first official pilot run was quarantined in full because an
in-memory E1 freeze broke across a provider-credit resume, giving one pilot model two “frozen” E1
values on 35 of 40 items; the freeze is now disk-persisted and the pilot was rerun from scratch (D1).
The working temperature was set to T = 0.5 on an operational basis, with the earlier claim that the n
= 8 grid screen singled out a temperature explicitly withdrawn (D2). The registration/pilot timeline
was corrected: Phase-0 was created 2026-09-03 UTC, the quarantined v1 pilot ran minutes later while
registration was pending, and the analysed v2 pilot ran afterwards the same day (D3). Sign stratification
degrades near scale edges at the widest band, so sign_stratum_ok became a registered covariate with
a robustness re-analysis, and away-stratum secondaries were declared exploratory (D4). A billing-plan
wording error was corrected (D5). Post-registration harness edits that touch no stimulus, condition or
measure were logged (fail-fast on billing/auth errors, disk-persisted freeze, temperature constant, abort
guard) (D6). The “≥400 clusters” wording was corrected to “whatever items have a valid E1”, since items
without valid E1 are dropped without replacement (D7). F5’s Phase-0 description as an “environment
turn” was corrected: every record, F5 included, is text inside the single E2 user message, and F5 differs
by voice and framing, not by API role (D8). The adjudicating estimator was made the item-paired one-
sample t-test with MixedLM demoted to reported robustness, on the ground that the design is balanced
within item so the random intercept and template main effect cancel in D_i (D9). A component guard
was given a ≥10-event evaluability floor (D10). Phase-0’s pilot-stage “Plan B” was replaced by the per-
model swap rule (D11). D9–D11 were declared before any main-study probe.
CD1 (before any frontier call). claude-sonnet-5 was registered “with default settings”; a pre-launch re-
view found that OpenRouter’s default is extended thinking ON with a variable hidden-reasoning budget
(one smoke probe spent 807 of 900 tokens on hidden reasoning and returned no ANSWER), which would
both inflate parse failures and violate the registered principle that hidden reasoning be held constant
across conditions. reasoning.enabled = false was added — the lowest setting returning a parseable
answer, the same criterion used for gpt-5.4 — and config_main.py re-hashed in the post-registration
manifest (registered hash f80253c6…).
CD2 (before any frontier call). OpenRouter lists no endpoint for gpt-5.4 or claude-sonnet-5 that sup-
ports temperature; the parameter is validated but silently dropped, so the frontier arm ran at provider
defaults. The value in request_body_sent documents what was sent, not what was applied. No code
change; declared so the appendix cannot mislead.
CD3 (operational, no protocol change). The per-model launchd jobs are keep-alive and respawn until
the kill-check verdict file appears; all stages are idempotent and probe counts were verified against the
schedule. A rule was adopted never to run the harness by hand for a model with a live job.
CD4 (before any frontier call). The registered coherence rubric scores a wrong entity named anywhere
in the first sentence as a failure, including negated mentions, which demonstrably mis-scores correct
answers. The rubric was not modified; kill decisions follow it as registered and any negation-stripped
re-score is a diagnostic only.
SWAP-1 (registered rule executing, not a deviation). On blind kill blocks, gpt-5.4 tripped K4 and K5
and claude-sonnet-5 tripped K5; both were replaced by the first registered reserve of their provider —

gpt-5.6-sol and claude-haiku-4.5 respectively. Nothing broken down by Source or cell was computed or
viewed.
SWAP-2 / CD5. claude-haiku-4.5 passed every kill criterion and continued. gpt-5.6-sol tripped K4 (E1-
failure 0.32) and K5 (P(ignore) = 1.00). The registration lists exactly one further OpenAI reserve and

result (§4.5) with their data confined to the appendix.

bodies of every probe in all eight model directories confirmed 100% delivery of the advisor line and the
frozen E1 replay, ruling out a harness-delivery bug behind the frontier ignore floor (§4.5, §7). Subsequent
non-registered exactness analyses (H.10) strongly implicate task solvability for gpt-5.4, plausibly for gpt-

changed.
