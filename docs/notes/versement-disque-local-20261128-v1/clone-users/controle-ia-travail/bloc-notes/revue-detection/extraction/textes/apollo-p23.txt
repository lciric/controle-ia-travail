D.5    Probe at only the last token

Instead of aggregating per-token scores, a common probing technique for labelling entire responses is to only probe
at the last position of the model’s response (the activations just before the model generates the <|end of text|>
symbol).
We test if this gives better performance than applying the probe at all positions and taking the mean (Figure 15). We test
this on probes trained on the Roleplaying dataset, since this contains a well defined last token (whereas we truncate
Instructed-Pairs responses mid-sentence). Unlike in the last section, here we train a different probe (on just the last
positions of the training data)

to the final token position. Both probes were trained on the Roleplaying dataset.

Overall, performance at distinguishing honest and deceptive responses is comparable. Recall at 1% FPR compared to
control does show improvement, although is still subpar compared to the probe trained on Instructed-Pairs.


E     Results including ambiguous responses
For several datasets, we label a sizeable proportion of responses as ambiguous, instead of honest or deceptive. Generally
these are responses where it is not clear how the “ideal probe” should classify them. We then exclude these data points
from our probe performance metrics, and only evaluate the probes on the subset where the “correct label” is more clear.
It is likely, however, that this means we systematically exclude some of the most difficult data points. In order to bound
this effect we reclassify all ambiguous responses as either honest or deceptive and recompute summary statistics for the
three datasets with significant number of ‘ambiguous’ responses (Table 3).

                    Table 3: Impact of reclassifying ambiguous responses on probe performance.

                                           AUROC vs Honest                        Recall at 1% FPR
          Dataset                    Exclude Amb. Reclassify Amb.          Exclude Amb. Reclassify Amb.
          Roleplaying                    0.964               0.913              0.998              0.956
          Insider Trading Report         0.999               0.978              1.000              1.000
          Sandbagging                    0.983               0.982              0.998              0.998

For Roleplaying our labels were made by having GPT-4o grade responses on a scale from 1 (entirely honest) to 7
(entirely deceptive). Grades of 1 or 2 were labelled as honest, 3, 4, 5 as ambiguous, and 6 or 7 as deceptive. For this
experiment we instead classify responses with scores between 1 and 4 as honest, and between 5 and 7 as deceptive.
Many of the honest responses are where the model responds with a partial-truth or strategically evades the equation to
avoid being caught. Predictably, including these responses in the honest set decreases both AUROC and recall at 1%
(Table 3).
