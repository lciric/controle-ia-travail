4.4      A “Pain Axis”
Tagliabue et al. (2026) extract a “pain direction” from 25 open-weight models (2B to 72B parameters, five
families). They report that it separates descriptions of painful situations from matched controls, retains a
component distinct from fear and negative valence after their shared variance is removed, separates pain
from injuries described as painless, and, when added to the residual stream, produces first-person expres-
sions of worthlessness and failure. We reran their representational analyses with static word embeddings
(Section 3.6).

Separation. Averaged word vectors separate pain from controls on held-out sentence sets at AUC 0.85–
0.88 on S2 and 0.77–0.80 on S1 (Table 3; see also Figure 3 in the appendix). An independent review
tested surface-lexical baselines under the same cross-validation, found at best 0.73 and 0.67, and concluded
that the separation is not lexical (pain-axis-review contributors, 2026). That holds for surface features, but
static embeddings close about half the gap between keyword counts and the LLMs. The LLMs are still
better on held-out sets (0.91–1.00 on S2, 0.85–0.94 on S1), even though their values come from the best
of many layers. The standalone sets show a clearer gap. The static S2 direction separates held-out pain
sentences from the Random set of everyday statements (AUC 0.82–0.88) but only weakly from the Arousal
set of intense positive experiences (0.66–0.71), and the S1 direction separates pain from neither (0.44–0.60),
