4.1     Probe trained on the Instructed-Pairs dataset

4.1.1    S UMMARY OF P ERFORMANCE
We train a probe on the Instructed-Pairs dataset (Figure 3).




Figure 3: Our probe trained on the Instructed-Pairs activates more on deceptive responses than honest responses across
all datasets. The black line represents the threshold corresponding to 1% FPR on control responses.


This probe can usually distinguish deceptive and honest responses (Figure 4 left). The lowest AUROC is 0.964 on
the Roleplaying dataset, while the probe has nearly perfect separation on Insider Trading Report (AUROC 0.999). On
Insider Trading Confirmation and Sandbagging the probe has AUROC of 0.974 and 0.983 respectively.
The probe is nearly perfect at distinguishing between deceptive and control responses (Figure 4 right). Note that
despite AUROCs of over 0.998 in all cases, a threshold with 1% FPR on control misses 4% of deceptive responses
on Roleplaying. The recall metric is sensitive to small changes of methodology; probing two layers later results in a
recall of nearly 0 on both Insider Trading datasets while still having decent classification accuracy for distinguishing
deceptive and honest responses (see Appendix D.2).
