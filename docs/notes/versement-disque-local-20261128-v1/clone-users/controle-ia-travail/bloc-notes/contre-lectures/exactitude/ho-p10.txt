6 CONCLUSION
Summary. In this work, we find that scaling probes, in both capacity and the quantity and diver-
sity of their training data, lets them generalize from an off-policy dataset of contextual falsehoods to
detecting on-policy agentic scheming in SHADE-Arena, when paired with an appropriate prefilled
follow-up question. They outperformed a frontier black-box monitor (Appendix K.3), at a fraction
of the cost. They also scaled favorably with model capability, suggesting that probes will remain
effective for future models. We evaluated our probes in introspective settings, where black-box mon-
itors fail by design, and found they generalized well to these extremely out-of-distribution settings.
There are abundant areas for future work, including whether there is an eventual limit to the scaling
of white-box probes with data, and the limitations of model introspection, given that our probes can
only be as accurate as the model’s own belief.
Limitations. We have seen that probes are limited by the knowledge and beliefs of the model that they
are built on. In particular, if misalignment and damaging actions only arise due to a certain malicious
persona (Marks et al., 2026) activating, probing in situations where the default assistant persona is
active may not detect any risks. Thus, using probes for extremely ambitious pre-deployment auditing
is currently limited by a lack of research and understanding of model beliefs themselves.
Recommendations. Given recent loss-of-control incidents, it is evident that frontier developers’
monitoring practices are inadequate. To the extent to which this is motivated by cost or computa-
tional limitations, this work shows that white-box probes can provide approximately state-of-the-art
monitoring at a fraction of the cost of frontier black box monitors. As such, we strongly recommend
that any deployment of potentially risky models, such as frontier models with strong offensive
cybersecurity capabilties, be monitored with white-box probes.
