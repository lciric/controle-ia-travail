Limitations

All our results come from one model, Gemma-3-27BInstruct, which is a weak case for hidden-state valence: Berg
and Kaiser (2026) find it is one of two models out of seven
without a robust valence channel. Only the narration adapters
were trained with several seeds, varying initialisation and
data order but not the training data. Every other adapter is a
single run. Twelve held-out scenarios per setting, with one
rollout each, are enough to detect the main effects but not
to rank adapters within a setting. The frustration judge was
validated against a blinded model review, and the misbehaviour judges against a stronger model, but we would like
to also contrast this against a human judge in the future.
Some analyses were less constrained than the primary tests.
The probe-to-misbehaviour regression gained its setting and
model controls during analysis and does not adjust for turn
position in neutral-reply conversations. The identical-history

comparison of rejection and neutral histories was computed
after the fact. Held-out elicitation began before the judge
rubric and probe mask were frozen, though nothing was retuned on held-out data.
