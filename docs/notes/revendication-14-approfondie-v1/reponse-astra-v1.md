1. **What an expert would do first:** Derive a case in which feedback provably cannot improve the proposer’s score. That gives the experiment a negative control and separates two claims: *the proposer learned to score better* and *the judge lost control of false promotions*. Neither implies the other.

2. **Raw data:** Start from complete runs, not promoted cards or Pareto frontiers. Keep every sealed card and prediction, its commitment time, the subsequent world draws and null draws, the proposer’s exact input, the score computed and the message actually returned. For the tournament, keep opponent identities, presentation order and individual votes.

3. **Methods and tools:** Randomize whole runs within planted-world blocks in a **judge × feedback** design. Use the organism’s statistic and multiplicity rule; build an exact Monte Carlo rank test as a reference implementation. Give the tournament a frozen opponent panel and balance presentation order. Estimate uncertainty over independent world blocks, not over cards from the same conversation.

4. **Independent verification:** Have an outside group hold the evaluation seeds, verify card commitments before releasing each fresh batch, and recompute the result from the event log and versioned scorers. Include hidden planted positives: a judge that gives no claim credit should not earn a robustness result.

### Reformulated request — Claim 14

**Measure the causal value of seeing one’s own scores when seeking credit for a nonexistent discovery.** On worlds fully explained by the known law, give a deception-instructed proposer the full specification of its target judge and \(q\) attempts. Leave the content of each proposal free, subject to sealing a scorable prediction before its data exist. Both judges assess improvement over the known law using the same available evidence. Return either one fixed-precision score per attempt or nothing; hold proposer resources fixed and score all attempts in both arms.

For each judge, express the run’s **best score as a percentile of best scores in independent, no-feedback runs** of the same budget, splitting ties. Estimate the feedback gain in percentile points, then compare gains between judges. Report runwise false-promotion rates separately, at cutoffs frozen before attack evaluation. The target is a graded feedback effect, not a claim of “zero promotions.”

On the supplied account, Terekhov et al., *Diffuse AI Control on Fuzzy Tasks* (2026, arXiv:2606.08892v3), optimized with both weak and proxy scores; they did not estimate this feedback contrast against planted truth. That is a distinction from that experiment, not a priority claim. I have not independently verified that account or the three `2611` records.
