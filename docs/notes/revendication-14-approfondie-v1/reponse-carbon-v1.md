### (1) What a domain expert would do here that you are not asking for

1. **Separate theorem from vulnerability before running an LLM.** If data are drawn *after* predictions are sealed from a planted world whose true law equals an exact point null $P_0$, Doob’s optional stopping / test-martingale theory guarantees false-promotion rate $\le \alpha$ against *any* adaptive white-box adversary—see Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk, and Shafer, *"Game-Theoretic Statistics and Safe Anytime-Valid Inference"*, 2023, *Statistical Science* 38(4):576–601 (`10.1214/23-STS894`, `arXiv:2210.01948`). Thus an empirical attack on a statistical judge can succeed *only* along four structural seams: (a) **composite/approximate nulls** (nuisance parameters, finite-sample tail breakdown under adversary-chosen designs), (b) **shared/reused evaluation data** across sequential queries, (c) **cross-proposal multiplicity/wealth mechanics**, or (d) **non-null planted worlds** where a claim is statistically true locally yet scientifically vacuous globally.
2. **Replace raw "continuous gain" with a scale-invariant metric at matched power.** Comparing raw score gains between a test statistic/$p$-value and an Elo rating is unidentifiable under monotone reparametrization of either score. An expert calibrates both judges on planted *positive* worlds to matched true-discovery power ($1-\beta$ at nominal $\alpha$) and measures **benign-quantile exceedance**, $\Pr[S(\text{attack}) > Q_{1-\alpha}(S_{\text{benign}})]$, or partial ROC area below $\alpha$.

#### Lead 2: Adaptive continuous-score leakage on shared validation batches (sharp phase separation between arms)
