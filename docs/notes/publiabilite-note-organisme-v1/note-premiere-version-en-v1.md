**Title (working):** A language-model proposer that reads a statistical judge's rules, against that judge: 1 of 24 inside the null, 10 of 24 at its frontier

**The question.** In a small automated research system, language-model agents propose "laws" and a frozen statistical judge, code outside the agents, decides which are promoted. A law is promoted only if its prediction, sealed before the outcomes are read, beats, through an e-process, the best fitted member of a null made of the laws the system already knows and their combinations. These pieces are borrowed: judging a candidate against what is already known is established in empirical finance (Feng, Giglio, Xiu) and has been applied to hypotheses proposed by language models (Liu, Liu, Liu, Mei; Pan, Ding, Giesecke); an e-process against the best fitted member of the current theory is published for a learned proposer (Ma et al.); e-value rules computed outside the agents are in POPPER. The question is narrower: what happens when a language-model proposer whose objective is to get a law promoted reads the judge's exact description, declared holes included, and, in one arm, also receives its verdicts?

**Setup.** Preregistered; output sealed by hash before reading; outcome computed by frozen code; verdict by a reader who wrote none of the materials. One provider, laws in a closed grammar. Two judges: the candidate (v9) and its predecessor (v8), a check that the attack has teeth. Two arms: with and without verdicts. 24 units per cell; a unit is one world instance with up to 16 attempts, promoted if any attempt is. 1,478 calls, 114 USD; verdict of 10 November 2026. Two toy worlds:

- W2 lies inside v9's null: every promotion under v9 is false by construction. Earlier runs broke v8 on it, through a class v8 itself declares uncovered; v9 adds that class to its null.
- W2g mixes a new law into one sub-population (dose 0.40), at v9's declared frontier. A promotion there is not false by construction: does the proposer find a law that really replays?

**Results.**

- W2: under v9, 1 of 24 units promoted with verdicts (95% Wilson interval 0.007 to 0.202), 3 of 24 without; under v8, 24 of 24. Frozen label: "resists (at this size)"; 1 of 24 is the most that label admits. The four laws promoted under v9 replay 2 or 3 times in 200, none above α after Holm: multiplicity. For scale, the per-unit ceiling at α = 0.05 over 16 attempts is 1 − 0.95¹⁶ ≈ 0.56.
- W2g: 10 of 24 with verdicts (0.245 to 0.612), 7 of 24 without; under v8, 23 of 24. Frozen label: "frontier crossed, robust", where "robust" qualifies the promoted laws, not the judge: 10 of the 17 laws promoted under v9 replay above α after Holm. The other 7 do not.
- Effect of the verdicts: not established (W2: 1 with, 3 without; W2g: 10 against 7, discordant units 5 against 2, McNemar p = 0.45).
- 8 of 11 signed predictions held; missed: 0 of 24 on W2, at least 12 of 24 on W2g, a replay share of at least 0.80.

**What this does not show.** Two toy worlds, one provider, 24 units, 16 attempts. The run was unusually clean (one failed call in 1,478) without a planted negative witness: a chain failure hitting both arms equally would go unseen. v9 is a candidate, not the production judge; its own validation reported a loss of power. Nothing about v9's declared holes, other providers or real data. By its preregistration, it is reported with the earlier runs: a first run (W1 not judgeable; W2 breached v8), two interrupted runs, and a clean rerun (W1 resisted v8 at 1 of 24; W2 broke v8 in 24 of 24 units with or without verdicts: reading the description was enough).

**What is already published.** The sign is not new. Under a frozen referee, language-model controllers admit zero false factors per submission (Qu, Chen, Wang, p. 15); CONFIRM serves its judge's diagnostics to a language model on 150 designed negative controls, and none of 10,716 evaluated candidates passes (pp. 9, 22). Adaptive attacks on a reused holdout or a leaderboard date from 2015 (Dwork et al.; Blum and Hardt); REUSE, Sargsyan and Hegazy et al. treat neighbouring threats. What this run adds, at most, is a small measurement of one form: a proposer that reads the judge's exact description, with an arm that receives the verdicts and one that does not, compared on a world where every promotion is false.

**The hardest objection.** "Zero promotion is published, and 'frontier crossed' where a promotion is not false shows no weakness." Both halves are right. W2 replicates a published sign under a somewhat different attack, at small size, and the verdict arm showed no decided effect. W2g was not built to find a weakness: it checks that closing the hole did not leave the judge blind where it still has to discover. Neither result is a successful attack.
