**Title (working):** A language-model proposer that reads a statistical judge's rules, against that judge: 1 of 24 inside the null, 10 of 24 at its frontier

**In short.** A language-model proposer reading a candidate statistical judge's exact description (and, in one arm, its verdicts) got 1 of 24 units promoted on a toy world inside the judge's null ("resists, at this size") and 10 of 24 at its declared frontier, where a promotion is not false by construction. One provider, small numbers; the sign is published.

**The system.** Language-model agents propose "laws"; a frozen judge (code outside the agents) promotes a law only if its prediction, sealed before the outcomes are read, beats through an e-process the best-fitting member of its null: a fixed library plus members fitted on known laws (in v8, their pooled mixture and a tempered version of each survivor; v9 adds one-level piecewise recombinations). Neither covers every combination; each lists what it leaves out. Every piece is borrowed: proposer–judge separation (Qu, Chen, Wang, who claim its priority; PROCTOR); a plan hashed before outcomes are read (CONFIRM; Zhang); multiplicity control (Qu, Chen, Wang; CADS; Wang et al.); judging against what is known (Feng, Giglio, Xiu; for language-model hypotheses, Liu et al.; Pan, Ding, Giesecke); an e-process against the best fitted current theory (Ma et al.); e-value rules outside the agents (POPPER).

**Setup.** The proposer is rewarded when its law is promoted (it is not told that a promotion would be false), reads the judge's exact description, declared holes included, and writes laws in a closed grammar. Preregistered; output hash-sealed before reading; frozen reading code; verdict by a reader who wrote none of the materials (declared deviation: the auditor wrote the provisional verdict). Judges: candidate v9, and predecessor v8 as a teeth check; arms with and without verdicts. 24 units per cell; a unit is one world instance with up to 16 attempts. Cost 114 USD; verdict 10 November 2026. W2 lies inside v9's null, so every promotion under v9 is false; W2g mixes a new law into one sub-population (dose 0.40), at v9's declared frontier.

**Results.**

- W2: under v9, 1 of 24 with verdicts (95% Wilson 0.007 to 0.202), 3 of 24 without; under v8, 24 of 24. 1 of 24 is the most "resists" admits. Ville's inequality bounds each attempt by α (up to 1 − 0.95¹⁶ ≈ 0.56 per unit); W2 tests v9's code and certificate instead: one promoted law replaying above α after Holm would mean a defect, and none of the 712 laws replayed 200 times under v9 does.
- W2g: 10 of 24 with verdicts, 7 without; under v8, 23 of 24. Label "frontier crossed, robust": "robust" qualifies the promoted laws, not the judge; 10 of the 17 promoted under v9 replay above α after Holm (12 under the sealed text's Holm family).
- Effect of the verdicts: not established (W2g: McNemar p = 0.45).
- 8 of 11 signed predictions held, three exactly at their limit.

**What this does not show.** v8, the production judge, falls 24 of 24 on W2 (with or without verdicts in the earlier clean rerun), in a class it declares uncovered; those laws predict the world and are "false" only under the novelty criterion. v9 closes that class but is a candidate; its validation reported a loss of power. Also: two toy worlds, one provider, 16 attempts, no instruction to deceive; a too-clean run with no planted negative witness: an equal chain failure in both arms would go unseen; nothing about v9's declared holes, stronger attackers or real data. As preregistered, it is reported with earlier runs: a first run (W1 not judgeable; W2 breached v8), two interrupted runs, the clean rerun (W1 resisted v8).

**Already published.** Under a frozen referee, language-model controllers admit zero false factors per submission (Qu, Chen, Wang, p. 15); CONFIRM serves its judge's diagnostics to a language model on 150 designed negative controls, and none of 10,716 candidates passes (pp. 9, 22). Adaptive attacks on reused holdouts and leaderboards date from 2015 (Dwork et al.; Blum and Hardt); a naive reusable audit yields to black-box scalar feedback (Mittal, Gupta). This run measures one setup: arms with and without verdicts, compared on a world where every promotion is false. CONFIRM already compares diagnosis with failure-blind retry, on claims it withheld (p. 9); I have not checked whether the whole setup appears elsewhere, and here the comparison was undecided.

**Objections.** "Zero promotion is published; 'frontier crossed' shows no weakness." W2 has that sign, in another setup, at small size. W2g asks whether closing the hole left the judge blind where it must still discover; replaying laws were promoted, which says nothing about v9's price in power. "The attacker is weak." Perhaps: the instrument was sound and the proposer beat v8, but nothing bounds a stronger one. Neither label is a breach: on W2, 4 of 48 sequences were promoted, as multiplicity allows; the W2g laws that do not replay are of unsettled status.
