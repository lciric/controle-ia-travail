# Measuring whether a multi-agent idea-generation system finds rare, high-consequence ideas

**Context.** A team plans a system in which several language-model agents propose, criticise and select candidate scientific ideas (hypotheses, quantitative laws, lemmas, protocols). Before running it, they want to build and validate a harness for measurement and steering. The harness should make it possible to show, or to refute, that the system finds ideas that are "narrow and deep": rarely reached by ordinary search, yet of large consequence once found. The comparison is against honest baselines: random sampling in the same space of candidate ideas, a mechanical enumerator, and the strongest single model given the same budget.

**Questions.**

1. **Target.** How can "narrow" and "deep" be defined operationally, so that both can be measured without a language-model judge whose preferences may overlap with the generator's? Relative to what reference should narrowness be measured? How is depth told apart from rarity alone?
2. **Sources of truth.** Compare candidate sources of ground truth (synthetic worlds with planted truth, retrodiction with a temporal cutoff, formal proof, executable tests, or others): what each can and cannot establish about narrowness and depth, and how each fails.
3. **Comparison.** What primary quantity, unit of analysis, cost accounting and decision rule would support the claim "better than the baselines"? Which further comparisons, if any, are needed to attribute a difference to a specific part of the system?
4. **Problem choice.** How could one choose problems or regions where such ideas are more likely, and how would that choice itself be tested rather than assumed?
5. **Feedback.** Which measurements, if any, may be returned to the generator during search, and which must be withheld, so that the harness keeps measuring what it claims to measure?
6. **Validating the harness.** What must the harness demonstrate about itself before any costly run?
7. **Plan.** Order the experiments from cheapest to most expensive, with stopping criteria and the sample sizes they require.
8. **Limits.** What is out of reach of any such harness? Consider explicitly that no harness of this kind may be feasible, or that the target is ill-posed, and say what evidence would show either.
