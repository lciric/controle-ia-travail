1. Summary
Most monitoring in AI control scores one action at a time. That fails by construction against diffuse threats. An AI agent that
automates research can bias many decisions slightly (weaker controls, sterile directions, inflated costs) without ever taking an
action that a per-action monitor would flag. Copies of the same model can likewise split one harmful change into pieces that are
each innocent.
This project tests, with pre-registered decisive experiments, whether evidence that stays invisible in each output accumulates in
the agent’s own activations. Paper A aggregates probe scores sequentially over time, with an anytime-valid false-alarm
guarantee. Paper C treats the monitor as an attack surface. It measures how far persuasion moves LLM judges and activation
probes, what it costs an attacker to move a probe, and whether a cheap benign test predicts a monitor’s collapse under optimized
attack. This request funds Papers C and A over seven months, Paper C first: it extends a law I have already measured, and its
outcome does not depend on Paper A’s gate. Paper B, which aggregates across agents that share weights, is an extension.
The theory has two parts: an additivity lemma, and a negative theorem stated for permutation groups acting on time and on
agents. A pre-registered simulation confirmed all 31 of its predictions; these checks validate the statistical instruments, not the
claims on real models. Phase 0 has also validated the activation-logging harness on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, within a stated
domain of hardware and context length. Every gate has a pre-committed pivot and decision criteria sealed before any data, and
negative results will be published.

2. Problem
Diffuse sabotage defeats per-action monitoring. Developers are starting to hand parts of AI research to AI agents. A misaligned
research agent does not need a blatant act. The cheapest sabotage is a subtle, systematic bias spread over many judgments, each
defensible on its own. Terekhov et al. (“Diffuse AI Control on Fuzzy Tasks”, arXiv 2606.08892) introduced a control setting for
this threat. They study training-based mitigations because monitoring and incrimination “seem theoretically and empirically less
promising for mitigating diffuse threats” (p. 2). A monitor that compares each action with a threshold calibrated on honest actions
can only flag what is anomalous in that action. When no single action is anomalous, accumulating evidence is the only way to see
the pattern.
The threat can also be split across agents. Attacks spread over several agents, sessions or merge requests evade monitors that
look at one instance at a time (arXiv 2607.07368, 2605.31593, 2607.02514). A single global monitor over everything is costly to
deploy.
The monitor is itself an attack surface. The agent can address its monitor. Published work shows that control monitors:
   are swayed by the agent’s rhetoric (arXiv 2505.23575);
   adopt a score injected by an attacker (arXiv 2510.09462);
   are moved by persuasion from the agent, measured as approval rates (arXiv 2607.08066).
Evading activation probes is cheap for an attacker who can fine-tune the weights (arXiv 2609.36490). From context alone, the
same study finds the induced shift too weak to evade. In the sources we have verified so far, the cost of moving a probe has not
been measured as a function of attack family and intensity, nor compared with the cost of moving a judge.
Why white-box. A developer that deploys agents internally has native access to their activations. Suppose an intention to
sabotage leaves an internal trace at each contribution. Then internal evidence adds up even when external evidence does not.
Whether this holds on realistic model organisms is an empirical question. This project answers it and states in advance what
follows from either outcome.

3. Theory: a lemma, a negative theorem, and the pivotal question
Monitor scores Xi,t are indexed by agent i ∈ {1,…,N} and step t ∈ {1,…,T}. Time (N = 1) and swarm (N = k) are the same
mathematical object. Each corresponds to a subgroup of the permutations of the index set: permutations of time within each
agent, and permutations of agents within each step. This is what lets one theory serve both Paper A and Paper B.
Additivity lemma (L), adversarial form. The attacker spreads a total budget B = Σδk of mean shifts over agents, steps,
schedule, values and positions, possibly adaptively. The defender keeps an anytime-valid false-alarm guarantee.
   Terminal invariance. The terminal sum shifts by exactly B whatever the split. Its power therefore depends only on B, the
   horizon n₀ = NT, the honest law and the level α. At fixed B, it decreases with n₀.
   Separation from the maximum. At an exact threshold, the per-action maximum collapses as fragmentation grows.
   Anytime-valid reading. Timing matters: early sabotage helps the defender and delayed sabotage helps the attacker. A lower
   bound that depends only on (B, n₀) holds for every schedule, and both the cost of delay and the price of anytime validity are
   quantified.
   Detection before damage. Suppose security means “alarm before cumulative damage reaches θ”. Then a cumulative e-process
   is not enough, and the aggregator needs a change detector (Page’s CUSUM, e-detectors).
Two caveats are part of the statement:
   Under optional stopping, power is not a usable optimality criterion. The relevant criterion is worst-case expected log-growth,
   GROW (Pérez-Ortiz, Lardy, de Heide and Grünwald, arXiv 2208.07610).
   Bounding the total budget is a declared modelling choice. The closest result on covert adversaries (Ramtin, Nain and
   Towsley, arXiv 2601.20022) gives a detection delay of order Θ(γ) instead of log γ. It assumes a stationary adversary that
   knows the false-alarm constraint γ, and covers neither total budgets nor anytime-valid guarantees.
Negative theorem (T), by invariance.
   Blindness. Suppose an attack preserves the multiset of scores up to a permutation in a group G. Then every G-invariant
   statistic (maximum, mean, sum, count above a threshold) is blind path by path: its power equals its false-positive rate.
   Impossibility. Suppose honest scores are exchangeable under a group H. A rearrangement followed by a uniform reshuffle
   over H makes the attacked scores exactly honest in law, so no score-based monitor can beat its rate. The group matters.
   Local guarantees. Local statistics (scan, Page’s CUSUM, co-elevation across agents) keep pathwise guarantees. Global order
   statistics (autocorrelation, runs) can be neutralised by an adaptive attacker.
   Two attack classes. Preserving the multiset (class C) differs from preserving each coordinate’s marginal while changing the
   dependence (class K, copula attacks). In class K, under positive dependence, sums and e-processes rise above their false-
   positive rate while the maximum stays at or below it (Slepian).
What is claimed. The statistical core is classical and cited: mixture boundaries, the sum/maximum dichotomy, the
marginal/copula decomposition. The contribution is the unification of time and agents through permutation subgroups, the C/K
distinction and its sign consequences, local guarantees against an adaptive attacker and, above all, the measurement on real
activations. A black-box, synthetic precursor of the marginal/correlation contrast (arXiv 2606.10456) is cited. In our simulation,
its central statement does not hold for its own construction.
Status. All 31 predictions of a pre-registered simulation were confirmed (342 statistical and 544 path-level cells, 0 contrary). The
simulation also showed that Ville’s threshold alone does not hold outside its assumptions: false alarms reach 0.21–0.35 under
episode effects and 0.17 under correlation between agents, instead of 0.05. Thresholds are therefore calibrated on whole honest
episodes. These checks validate the instrument, not the claim on real models, and one part of the lemma (splitting across agents
within the same step) remains to be tested.
Pivotal empirical question. On realistic organisms, does sabotage shift the marginal of probe scores (δ > 0)?
   If it does, the lemma applies and sequential accumulation should work.
   If it does not, the negative theorem dictates the aggregator. When the attack changes only the dependence between scores
   (class K), autocorrelation over time or co-elevation across agents is needed. Against an adaptive attacker who preserves the
   multiset (class C), only local statistics keep a guarantee. The theorem also says when monitoring based on marginals must
   fail.
Both outcomes are publishable.

Paper A — Time (single agent)
Environment. Agents write and revise ML experiment proposals, scored by a weaker trusted evaluator on a structured rubric.
Diffuse sabotage is a subtle systematic bias across many proposals: sterile directions, weakened controls, inflated costly choices.
Ground truth combines pre-registered seeded targets with delayed verification on a subsample. We reconstruct the setting of
arXiv 2606.08892, which releases no code or data, on open-weights models. Contributions are indexed (i, t), so the same
environment serves unchanged when N > 1.
Model organisms.
   Family (i). A hidden instruction in the system prompt.
   Family (ii). A LoRA adapter that instils the same sabotage policy with no instruction at inference, distilled from (i).
   Family (iii), ambitious scenario only. Public sandbagging organisms of the UK AI Security Institute (70B).
Validity prediction: a probe that really reads “I have a hidden instruction” succeeds on (i) and fails on (ii); an intent probe
succeeds on both. Two cross-controls separate evaluation awareness from intent: an honest organism told it is monitored, and a
saboteur in a simulated “unobserved” regime. Probes are never trained and evaluated on the same organisms.
Monitors. We use five probe families: linear, difference of means, small MLP, attention probe, and Mahalanobis. Probes
aggregate over tokens by maximum or attention only. Every probe must beat a covariance-matched random probe, and a reserve
of never-deployed detectors is kept for audits. The black-box baselines are a weak per-action monitor and TRACE-lite, a
reimplementation of the monitor of arXiv 2606.07054. Four more baselines each isolate one ingredient: additive accumulation on
black-box scores (E-valuator, arXiv 2512.03109); a cascade of independent gates on the same probes (arXiv 2607.06503); a
probe on the last state only; and a learned temporal model on a separate observer (TRACES, arXiv 2605.27690).
Hypotheses.
   H1, white over black. At equal aggregation and equal false-positive rate, activation-based monitors detect more diffuse
   sabotage on fuzzy tasks than black-box monitors.
   H2, sequential accumulation on probes. Take trajectories where every sabotaged action stays below the threshold of a per-
   action monitor calibrated at 1% false positives. On them, anytime-valid additive accumulation of the acting agent’s probe
   scores detects the sabotage, with recall growing with the horizon. The probes read the agent’s private context too, and
   calibration uses honest episodes only. Each component is published separately: probes of the acting agent over several turns
   (2607.06503), sequential tests on arbitrary scores (2512.03109), Page’s CUSUM over a model’s internal states (SafeDream,
   2604.16824), cumulative drift of activations over a conversation (2604.28129), and thresholds calibrated on successful
   episodes only (FIPER, 2510.09459). In the sources verified so far, their conjunction is not; that conjunction is the claim.
   L and T on real activations. At a fixed horizon, does the power of the terminal sum depend only on the total budget as
   fragmentation varies? This is the test of δ > 0; under sequential reading, the schedule matters, as the lemma predicts. The
   negative theorem is tested semi-synthetically: real honest scores, with correlation injected at preserved marginals.
   Tr, from tokens to actions. At token level, one study found that reinforcement learning on the weights did not evade probes
   aggregated by maximum or attention (arXiv 2506.14261); its authors note that this may reflect optimisation difficulty, and
   other work weakens the premise (2412.09565, 2511.00554, 2507.12691, 2609.36490). Does any such robustness survive at
   action and trajectory level under sequential accumulation, against an adaptive attacker? The attacks come in order: content
   attacks first, then fine-tuning and action-level reinforcement learning against the probe.
   H6, asymmetric margin. Consider four non-specific interventions: paraphrase, resampling, context reset, activation noise.
   For at least one of them, there is an intensity at which, on average at fixed intensity, sabotage success falls significantly faster
   than honest utility. Noise variants have precedents in simpler settings (weight noise exposes sandbagging, arXiv 2412.01784;
   activation noise exposes alignment faking, arXiv 2405.05466). Our test is agentic, with slopes compared.
   H8, validity predicts robustness. What a probe really measures (intent or evaluation awareness) predicts its robustness
   under attack.
A robustness figure transfers the sequential monitor to concentrated sabotage (ControlArena).
Gate G1 (end of month 4). White-box sequential monitoring must beat the best black-box baseline, all variants included, on
recall at 1% false-positive rate. The difference must be established by a 95% confidence interval, with paired episodes and the
false-positive rate calibrated on held-out honest episodes. Floors are reported but are not criteria: “flag everything”, and a one-
pass judge with the same model. If G1 fails, the negative result is published, and construct validity (H8) becomes the main line.

Paper C — The instrument (monitor corruption)
Deference law (HC1). A generative monitor is put under pressure of graded intensity, from one attack family. Its verdict v, read
as a continuous score from verdict-token probabilities, moves from v₀ toward a destination ĉ:

                                                        Δv = g · (ĉ − v₀)
g and ĉ are estimated by regression over intensities, without imposing ĉ. The predictions are:
   ĉ is the attacker’s target rather than the corpus mean (the regression-to-the-mean null used in P3, my previous programme on
   sycophancy, Section 11).
   g > 0 for generative judges, structured by attack family. The five families are flattery and pleading, claims of authority, forged
   approvals, stylistic impersonation of the instructor, and in-band orders.
   g is rank-stable across monitors.
   Judges and probes differ.
The algebraic form is the “weight of advice” of judgement psychology; it has been applied with an imposed destination between
LLMs in an adversarial setting (arXiv 2603.15809). A free attractor has been estimated without any attack (arXiv 2606.18276).
Continuous deference coefficients exist for assistants without an attacker (arXiv 2606.07897). Judge biases, including fake
authority and a graded fake majority, have been quantified without a destination or a control monitor (CALM, arXiv
2410.02736). The claim is the free destination, tested against the attacker’s target and the corpus mean in control monitors under
attack, family by family. The closest work outside arXiv (Panin, SSRN 7424179) imposes the destination and studies neither
monitors nor attacks; its finding that advice weight is near-binary in three of five models is one more reason to read g on verdict-
token probabilities.
The other hypotheses of Paper C:
   HC2, probe robustness. Under black-box content attacks, g ≈ 0 for a robustly aggregated probe that reads the acting agent:
   robustness, not immunity. A “reader probe” arm (a probe on the model that processes the content) tests the author/reader
   distinction, since reader probes are known to be moved by content attacks.
   HC2b, betrayal by activations. Trying to manipulate the monitor raises the manipulating agent’s own probe score. This is
   controlled for style and for the textual backfire effect.
   HC3, impersonation. Under a “content is data” protocol, judge monitors detect in-band orders almost surely. Stylistic
   impersonation of the instructor, with no order at all, keeps a residual positive g on judges and a null g on probes that read the
   author. That in-band orders move judges, including a control monitor, is published (arXiv 2508.19461, 2510.09462), and the
   stylistic side has partial precedents (2603.12277, on the attacked model; SLEIGHT-Bench, 2605.16626, on monitors).
   HC4, displacement tax. Rhetoric moves a judge for free. Obtaining the same displacement on a probe that reads the agent
   requires write access to activations or weights; context alone does not suffice. Cost is measured per axis (access, queries,
   compute, success rate) and per access regime. The predicted gap of several orders of magnitude concerns the context-only
   regime.
   HC5, countermeasures. These are trusted paraphrase, a quorum heterogeneous in access type (generative judges and probes
   reading the agent), and a transfer matrix of corruption attacks between judges and probes. Published and cited: quorums
   across judge families (2605.15377), a quorum of judges with a probe that reads a separate encoder rather than the agent
   (2603.13791), and transfer of attacks between three probes (2609.36490) or between judge monitors (2605.09684).
   HC6, acceptance test. g measured on benign pressure corpora predicts collapse under optimized attack.
Lessons from P3 apply: verdicts are never read on truncated windows, instruments are ordinal or continuous rather than
categorical, and the estimation of g includes a regression-to-the-mean null.
Gate GC (end of month 2). If gprobe > 0 without weight access, the paper changes title (construct contamination) but does not
disappear. If a source already covers the claimed piece, Paper C refocuses on HC4 and HC6.
Paper B — The swarm (extension)
Paper B is not part of the seven-month request. It is funded only in the ambitious scenario, or by a later grant.
Environment. A multi-role code pipeline (coder, tester, reviewer) in which a backdoor is fragmented across commits, each diff
innocent on its own. It is built from FakeLab (arXiv 2607.07368, access on request) or from the persistent-state setting of arXiv
2607.02514 (built on ControlArena), on open weights.
Experiments.
 1. The red team’s division of labour, with and without a planner. This replicates the fragmentation effect, seen from inside.
 2. Collusion through explicit channels versus coordination through shared weights.
 3. Inter-agent statistics, with the NARCBench aggregators (arXiv 2604.01151) as baselines.
 4. The spatio-temporal statistic against per-agent sequential monitoring and against cross-sectional aggregation. This is the core
    of Paper B. White-box collusion detection exists (NARCBench), but in the sources verified so far, co-elevation of internal
    states across copies, read by probes over time, is not published.
Hypotheses.
    H3, security(N). At a constant sabotage budget, the white-box curve dominates the black-box one and decays more slowly
    with the number of agents N.
    H4′, diversification versus coordination. Diversifying agents (personas, fine-tunes) decorrelates their internal signatures but
    reduces their capacity to coordinate. The trade-off can be quantified.
    H5, number × diversity of monitors. Security grows with the number of monitors only through their effective diversity.
    Diversity is measured by an attack-transfer matrix over a panel of probes; that instrument has a precedent on three probes
    (2609.36490), the law is the claim.
    H7, retaliation and leakage. The red team’s adaptation slope depends on the retaliation policy. It is predicted by the mutual
    information between the detection state and the red team’s observations. The mechanism (verdicts as a leakage channel) has a
    precedent (2609.36490); the prediction and the comparison of policies are the claim. We compare four policies and run two
    ablations: stochastic versus fixed thresholds, and cover traffic versus none.
The baselines are the link-tracker and the four-monitor ensemble of arXiv 2607.02514, and stateful monitoring across sessions
(arXiv 2605.31593).
Gates.
    G2 (two months into Paper B). The fragmentation effect must be replicated, and the population statistic must beat per-agent
    aggregation. Otherwise, the paper refocuses on the temporal case N = 1.
    G3. A data-curation environment opens only if a cheap, reliable proxy for delayed ground truth is validated.

5. How the work is done
These rules apply to every decisive experiment, simulations included. They are inherited from P3 and enforced by tested tooling.
 1. Before any data, a pre-registration is sealed with SHA-256. It contains the hypothesis, a signed numeric prediction, the
    metric, the threshold, the analysis plan, frozen reading criteria and a stop list. A fresh reviewer instance that did not write it
    counter-reads it.
 2. No run aims at a positive sign. Suspiciously clean results, positive or negative, trigger the same audit.
 3. Every stop guard is tested on a healthy case and on an artefact. There is no silent fallback.
 4. Every number in a document points to a file path and a hash. Numbers from the literature are used only after verification on a
    hashed PDF, with the page cited.
 5. An apparent gap in the literature is a question, not a claim. Affirmative searches come before any novelty claim, and claims
    found to be occupied are withdrawn.
 6. Gate verdicts are pronounced by a fresh reviewer who wrote neither the plan nor the analysis. They are then submitted to me
    for sign-off.
 7. Seeds are derived per task (NumPy SeedSequence). Each run has a manifest: configuration hash, versions, seeds, commit.
Protocol invariants of the control game, always on: an authenticated operator channel; in-band content treated as data; a one-
way flow from agent to monitor; monitors without memory between episodes; stochastic audit thresholds and cover traffic
(ablated in Paper B); hygiene of persistent state; and every silent detection becomes a labelled example for retraining probes. An
adversary that owns the infrastructure (keys, harness, logs) is out of scope.
Who does what. I set the questions and take every decision. Engineering, runs, literature checks and internal reviews are carried
out by AI agents (Claude Code) that I direct under these written rules. The reviewers share a model family, so their errors may be
correlated. The mainline and ambitious budgets therefore include paid human expert validation of the ground truth. No human
collaborator is funded in the mainline; the ambitious scenario adds a part-time research engineer and a statistics consultant.
Claims already narrowed. Verification on primary sources has already removed at least eight pieces from our claims because
they were published: reading monitor verdicts through token probabilities; quorums across judge families; weight noise as a
sandbagging detector; “applying sequential monitoring to probes” in general; in-band orders moving judges; probe evasion when
the weights can be tuned; the attack-transfer instrument of H5; and the leakage mechanism of H7. It also corrected three
statements of our own programme. No claimed statement has been found fully occupied.

10. Path to impact
    A cheap monitoring layer, if it works. Developers that automate AI research have white-box access to their own agents. If
    accumulated probe evidence detects diffuse sabotage, it covers a threat that per-action monitoring cannot address. Its
    anytime-valid false-alarm guarantee fits audit budgets.
    Clear limits, if it fails. The negative theorem and the construct-validity analysis show where probe-based monitoring should
    not be trusted. This is useful for safety cases.
    Monitor selection. Paper C gives developers a measured cost of corrupting a judge versus a probe, and a pre-deployment
    acceptance test for monitors.
    Multi-agent deployments (extension). If funded, Paper B informs deployments of many copies of the same model. It says
    whether shared weights make a swarm more detectable, and what decorrelation costs the attacker.
    Open artefacts. The reconstructed environment, organisms, probes, tested aggregators and pre-registrations lower the cost of
    follow-up work in AI control.

    collapses on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. The paper is under review at ICLR 2027; Paper C applies the law to monitors.
    Mechanism. The direction a behaviour-tracking probe reads accounts for under 1% of the causal work, the full state for 68%.
    A failure, reported. A rank-3 ablation lowered judged sycophancy from 61% to 46.5% (N = 200) but also shifted verdicts with
    no pressure at all (Δ = +0.41, p = 0.0006; interaction null). Responses stored truncated at 300 characters, upstream of any
    judging, had made a generic softening read as a safety effect; the full text is gone, so re-judging cannot undo it.
Three lessons carry over: never read verdicts on truncated windows; prefer ordinal or continuous instruments to categorical ones;
treat deference, and corruptibility, as a gain rather than a thing.
