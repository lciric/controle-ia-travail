## Jan Kirchner (Anthropic) — project 2, automated alignment researchers

**Why this project.** Since October I have been building a small automated-research system relevant to your project (public: github.com/lciric/ideation-organism): agents from several providers propose hypotheses on AI control, a frozen statistical judge (sealed bets, e-values) scores them, and preregistered "death tests" run in a sandbox, checked by a model of another family. Its design question is yours — does the method resist gaming the setup — and I have started attacking it myself. I wrote 27 scripted cheaters against my own judge: four that recopy or blend already-promoted laws got through, until the judge was given a null fitted on every known law; that null is now its only one, and its preregistered validation (1,000 repetitions per cheater, checked by a fresh reader) held all 11 fatal cheats at 0 of 1,000 for a power cost of at most 0.02. A counter-reading found that until v56 no code read the death-test verdicts; the verdict now comes only from the sealed raw file and the preregistered statistic, never from the experimenter's script. On real data the instrument reports its limits rather than findings: on real episodes the judge says "not enough power", and a power curve shows why; in a real two-family round the LLM judges sat at their ceiling (11 of 12 cards "notable", κ = 0).[[T05]]

**What I would do.** First reproduce the system on one task suite of your harness (or port its guards onto the harness), then run a preregistered ablation of judge freezing and cross-family verification under one explicitly incentivised gaming attack (agents rewarded for passing the judge rather than for being right), with the cheaters bench as a reusable adversarial test of the judge. Primary endpoint: the fraction of accepted hypotheses that pass held-out, execution-based tests. Confirmatory test: generalisation to a second task suite. Stretch goal: scaling to larger models. Deliverable: a preprint, the task suite and the evaluation code.

**Relevant experience.** Two years of hash-audited research with sealed preregistrations (a 131-object claims register, including 19 refuted claims and 29 retained failed predictions); mutation benches on the system's own guards (224 deliberate code mutations, each killed for the expected reason; 929 tests); the control programme (white-box monitors for diffuse threats; summary in my writing sample); a research master's in computational neuroscience (ENS, Ostojic lab; PLOS Computational Biology 2023).
