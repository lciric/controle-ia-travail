## 8. Jérémy Scheurer (Apollo Research) — ideas spreading through agent swarms; loss-of-control metrics

**Why this project.** Your first option — how ideas spread through agent swarms and how a swarm drifts into misalignment — is the second paper of my control programme and the system I have been running since October. The programme treats time and agents as the same object: monitor scores indexed by agent and step, with permutation subgroups (time within an agent, agents within a step), so that, within that invariance framework, what is proved for one agent over time applies to many agents at once. The negative theorem says which monitors a swarm can make blind by spreading a threat over its members; the open question is whether internal evidence (activations) still accumulates when the external evidence does not. On the "ideas spreading" side I have a small automated-research system (public: github.com/lciric/ideation-organism): agents from several providers propose ideas, a frozen judge scores, death tests kill, a verifier of another family checks; its registry records how ideas propagate, get copied and get killed — a pilot data source for the kind of trace you describe, not evidence of misalignment propagation.

**What I would do.** The propagation project, not the loss-of-control metric as well. In one message-board environment, seed a defined misleading or misaligned proposal in one agent and measure adoption, persistence and task-relevant consequences across a small population, with ground truth and non-seeded controls. Primary, preregistered comparison: per-agent monitoring against one swarm-level statistic (co-elevation across agents); activation monitors added only if the behavioural effect is robust. Deliverable: a preprint and a reusable environment. Daily use of Claude Code and Codex is how I work; available full time in London.

**Relevant experience.** The control programme (theory, preregistered simulation with 31 confirmed predictions, external security review); the automated-research system; the sycophancy and probe pipelines.
