## Jérémy Scheurer (Apollo Research) — ideas spreading through agent swarms; loss-of-control metrics

**Why this project.** Your first option — how ideas spread through agent swarms and how a swarm drifts into misalignment — is the second paper of my control programme. The programme treats time and agents as one object: monitor scores indexed by agent and step, with permutation subgroups (time within an agent, agents within a step), so that, within that invariance framework, what is proved for one agent over time applies to many agents at once. The negative theorem says which monitors a swarm can make blind by spreading a threat over its members. A preregistered simulation (1 November, synthetic scores) shows the shape of it: per-agent monitoring is blind when agents synchronise their sabotage in time, while a swarm-level statistic (co-elevation across agents at the same step) detects it, with an exact guarantee once half the swarm synchronises; and thresholds must be calibrated on whole honest swarms — calibrated on cells assumed independent, co-elevation raised false alarms on 62–83% of honest swarms under correlation between agents, against 5% intended. Its fresh reader noted that most of its 23 confirmed predictions were identities or closed forms, so I preregistered a second test on cells never computed and on the real honest score distribution of my first activation experiment: [[ESSAIM_HO]]. On the "ideas spreading" side, my small automated-research system (public: github.com/lciric/ideation-organism) records how ideas from agents of several providers are copied, merged and killed — a pilot data source for the kind of trace you describe, not evidence of misalignment propagation.

**What I would do.** The propagation project, not the loss-of-control metric as well. In one message-board environment, seed a defined misleading or misaligned proposal in one agent and measure adoption, persistence and task-relevant consequences across a small population, with ground truth and non-seeded controls. Primary, preregistered comparison: per-agent monitoring against one swarm-level statistic (co-elevation across agents), calibrated on whole honest swarms; activation monitors added only if the behavioural effect is robust. Deliverable: a preprint and a reusable environment. Daily use of Claude Code and Codex is how I work; available full time in London.

**Relevant experience.** The control programme (theory; preregistered simulations, including one on adaptive defences; external security review); the automated-research system; the sycophancy and probe pipelines.
