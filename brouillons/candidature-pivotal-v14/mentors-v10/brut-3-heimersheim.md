## 3. Stefan Heimersheim (Google DeepMind, interpretability) — activation plateaus

**Why this project.** Your plateau question — models robust to small activation perturbations, then changing fast beyond a threshold — is the shape of a result I keep running into from the other side. On Llama-3.1-8B, six rank-1 interventions on a linearly readable sycophancy direction (steering, directional ablation, SAE clamping, fine-tuning against the probe, head/MLP zeroing) leave the judged behaviour unchanged; a rank-3 subspace from SVD of activation differences at the decision point moves it (61% → 46.5%, N=200, p=0.002), direction-specific against a norm-matched random subspace. A zero-pressure control then showed the rank-3 shift to be generic — verdicts move with no social pressure applied. The probed direction tracks behaviour (ρ=0.68) yet does under 1% of the causal work versus 68% for the full state. This is a concrete starting point for testing whether behavioural insensitivity along probe-defined directions coexists with effects from a low-dimensional subspace; it does not yet establish a plateau or a threshold, because rank is not perturbation magnitude and no dose–response curve has been measured.

**What I would do.** On one model and the existing behavioural readout, preregister an operational definition of a plateau and measure dose–response curves over perturbation norm for probe-defined and SVD-defined subspaces, treating rank and norm separately, with random-subspace controls. Confirmatory test: replication of any threshold on held-out items. Extensions only, if time permits: whether plateau boundaries yield a feature dictionary different from the SAE's, and what crossing a plateau costs an attacker who evades a probe. Deliverable: a preprint and reproducible analysis.

**Relevant experience.** I have used TransformerLens, nnsight and SAE-lens in the projects above, including path-patching analysis across 1,024 heads and Goodfire SAEs; five mechanistic analyses of quantisation on GPT-2. I value direct empirical feedback and preregistered falsification.
