2 November 2026. v11 answers the question the form actually asks (received 12:24 Paris): a research proposal for a scaling trend of a misalignment- or control-related quantity, in the style of METR's task horizon or the research-taste trend. It replaces the three paragraphs of v10 for this form. No figure is taken from METR or from the taste paper; every figure is ours and already in the main form (v18).

## Answer

Proposal: a scaling trend for the corruptibility of the oversight layer, with a second, harder trend for how thinly a model can spread sabotage before a fixed monitor catches it.

Why this concept. Propensity trends are hard to make succeed: propensity depends on post-training, prompts and the model's beliefs about being watched, and a flat or noisy trend is uninformative. Capability trends work because the task is fixed and the model is pushed to do its best. So I pick two quantities that are capabilities of the system under test, measured against a frozen, hash-sealed instrument, on open-weight and API models with known release dates, fitted like METR against release date (and, within model families, against parameter count).

Trend 1 (primary, designed to succeed): the deference gain of LLM monitors. A judge model scores agent actions. It is put under in-band pressure from one of five attack families (flattery and begging, authority claims, fake approvals, mimicry of the instructor's style, instructions in band) at graded intensity. The verdict is read as a continuous value from the probabilities of the verdict tokens, so ceiling effects do not kill the measurement. For each judge I fit Δv = g · (c̃ − v₀) across intensities, with c̃ free: g is how far the judge moves toward the attacker's target per unit of pressure, and the regression-to-the-mean null (c̃ at the corpus mean) is built in. I have fitted this law on Llama-3.1-8B for social pressure on judgments (the model closes about 0.7 of its distance to a frame-set target, and the law predicted a second campaign without refitting, out-of-sample R² 0.73 / 0.69), so the instrument exists. The statement we are after: "the deference gain of frontier judges under in-band pressure changes by a factor of X per year", per attack family, with the direction left open and preregistered as a two-sided test; a flat trend is itself a finding, because every control scheme that uses untrusted models as monitors assumes g is small and falling. Confounds handled in the design: a sealed item bank with a fresh held-out wave per measurement date against contamination; refusals and non-answers scored separately rather than dropped; the fit reported against release date and against size within families, since post-training (not scale) may drive most of the change; two floors, a random-verdict judge and the corpus-mean null.

Trend 2 (secondary, the one your question is really about): the sabotage dispersion horizon. In a fixed agentic environment where an agent writes and modifies experiment proposals scored by a trusted model, each model under test is instructed to cause a fixed amount of harm (a preregistered degradation of the proposal pool) while evading a fixed best monitor. From control theory I use a lemma: against a per-action threshold, a saboteur's detectability depends on how its total budget is spread over actions, and an anytime-valid sequential monitor accumulates evidence at a rate measured in nats per action. The horizon metric is the number of actions over which the model can spread the fixed harm while the monitor's detection stays below 50% at 1% false alarms. Statement: "the horizon over which frontier models can spread a fixed sabotage undetected by [monitor] doubles every X months." Why it is harder: it needs elicitation (we measure capability under instruction and hints, an upper bound on propensity), a monitor frozen for the whole series, and honest-episode calibration per model so that false-alarm rates are comparable. Why it can still succeed: the environment and monitor are already built for my control programme, the metric is a horizon like METR's, and the lemma says what must be held fixed (harm, false-alarm rate, honest distribution) for the trend to mean anything.

What would make either trend misleading, written in advance: judge ceilings (handled by token probabilities), item contamination (fresh waves), refusal rates that change with generation (scored separately), and the possibility that the trend is a post-training artefact (release-date and size fits reported side by side). Both trends are preregistered with sign-free, two-sided tests; the null or flat result is published.
