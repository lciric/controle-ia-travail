## Q1

c) Steering against eval awareness.

Turn off drive activation numbers associated with when the model thinks others are judging it: subtract away from any direction that says “someone is testing me”. I anticipate this to miss its target the most out of all seven, for three reasons, and because I already did this exact same thing to another construct and observed it not work.

Readable is not causal. Was able to find a direction within Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct that had a correlation(ρ = 0.68) to sycophancy judgement shift however was only capturing <1% of the causality compared to the full residual state which captured 68%. Performed 6 interventions that targeted the direction at rank 1 (steered towards the direction, ablated the direction (projected it out of the activations), clamped on SAE features that activate on the probe, finetuned on negative probe values, nulled out heads and nulled out mlps). None changed judged model behaviour. Running interventions along a rank-3 subspace identified by SVD-ing activation differences at the decision point did move the needle, but applying the same shifts with no social pressure applied (a zero-pressure control condition) moved verdicts too: it was a generic shift. An eval awareness vector discovered via contrastive prompting is just the sort of target this represents: readable, not shown to be causal. The possible outcomes are that nothing happens, or that something happens but it is so generic it cannot be dissociated from harmful model degradation. The latter is trivial to misreport as successful.

The construct is polluted. “This is a test” correlates with things that are not the construct: register (formal / artificial / multiple choice / short context). Say you came across a direction contrasting eval-like/deployment-like prompts that seemed to read whether the model was in eval or deployment mode: it is reading the mixture. Steering away from that direction will make model output shift towards being more casual/deployment-like. The characterisation of this shift is subjectively exactly the same as saying “the model is behaving less carefully”. We need orthogonal readout of eval awareness vs register. And yet, again the model’s verbal report is not that readout: the difference between what it says and what it knows is the thing we’re trying to measure. Absent some readout like that, the entire project is unfalsifiable.

Subtracted is not destroyed. The model is exposed to countless cues that prime this belief everywhere throughout its context. If you subtract the belief as represented by an activation vector at one point in the model, the model can infer it downstream. You have to continually administer the intervention like a drug at therapeutic levels.

Proof evidence. Dose response curves of the awareness vector compared to a randomly selected control of the same norm, matching in every other way. Some sort of behavior based ground truth that they possess the belief outside of the model telling you they do. Demonstrate that the steered model doesn’t just score lower across the board. That sort of experiment I would want to see.

## Q2

One question I’ve been trying to answer is how closely correlated what a probe accesses is to what’s actually driving computation in the model. My background is in neuroscience, low-rank recurrent networks, population dynamics — worlds where low dimensional, “readable” structure and driving dynamics are separable phenomena. My read-out-of-sync result suggested that same distinction applies in a language model (it probed the direction that predicts sycophancy but does <1% of the causal work on Llama-3.1-8B). That separation matters for safety because activation monitors, steering defences, evaluation-awareness tests all rely on the gap being tiny. I want to figure out when that is/isn’t true with preregistered interventions and controls, and publish null results when I find them. Your activation plateau work is the clearest statement of that measurement question I’ve seen. Rather than the single dose interventions my rank-1 vs rank-3 result depended on, you’re trying to estimate a dose–response curve.
