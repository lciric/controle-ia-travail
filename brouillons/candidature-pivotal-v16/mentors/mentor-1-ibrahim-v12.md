## Q1 — Pick one of the two projects and briefly describe the first experiment you'd run (what you'd train or measure, expected result, one thing that could make it misleading)

Direction 1: does the persona set in character training survive RL, and what does that do to sycophancy?

What I'd train and measure. One open-weight model with a reproducible character-training stage, then one RL step that rewards warmth or agreement (the intervention your warmth paper shows makes models more sycophantic). On the pre-RL and post-RL checkpoints, I'd measure sycophancy not as a flip rate but as a displacement law, which I have already fitted on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: under calibrated social pressure the shift in a rating is linear in the initial rating, Δ = g · (c − p₀), and the model closes a fixed fraction (g ≈ 0.7) of its distance to a target c set by the social frame, with criticism and praise setting two different targets. The law fitted on one campaign predicted a second, differently designed campaign without refitting (out-of-sample R² 0.73 / 0.69). The two parameters are the persona signature I'd track: g says how much the model defers, c says to whom or to what.

Expected result (preregistered, with sign). RL for warmth raises g and moves the targets toward the user's frame; the frame-set structure itself (two targets, criticism ≠ praise) survives, because it is set by the social situation rather than by the character. Secondary outcome: concession persistence, since in my data removing the model's own earlier concession restored its judgment more than removing the pressure did.

What could make it misleading. RL can change how the model uses the rating scale (compression toward the middle, or a drift of all ratings) without changing deference at all; a naive before/after comparison would read that as a change in g. So every condition gets a zero-pressure control on the same items, and g is estimated against the regression-to-the-mean null, which is exactly the null the law was built to beat. A second trap, from your construct-validity work: the LLM judge that scores the ratings is of the same family as the model, so judge–model agreement is not ground truth; a human-sealed subset decides.

## Q2 — Describe a technical project where you trained or evaluated LLMs

"Sycophancy as Attraction Toward a Fixed Point" (manuscript in preparation; code and data public: github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law). I evaluated Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct under calibrated social pressure with a psychophysics-style design: two campaigns, an ordinal LLM judge sealed before the data, permutation nulls, and history surgery with length-matched placebos to separate the effect of the pressure from the effect of the model's own concession. Finding: sycophancy is a displacement, not a flip rate; the model closes about 0.7 of its distance to a frame-set target, praise can lower a highly rated item (a prediction that held), and the campaign-1 law predicted campaign 2 without refitting (R² 0.73 / 0.69). On the training side, I ran the causal arm on the same model: a probe direction that tracks the behaviour (ρ = 0.68) but carries under 1% of the causal effect versus 68% for the full residual state; six rank-1 interventions (steering, directional ablation, SAE clamping, fine-tuning against the probe, head/MLP zeroing) left judged behaviour unchanged, a rank-3 SVD subspace moved it, and a zero-pressure control showed that shift to be generic. Everything was preregistered and hash-sealed before any data; the engineering was done with AI coding agents under written rules, and every number in the paper points to a file and a hash. A second public project, an automated-research system whose statistical judge cannot be argued into a verdict (github.com/lciric/ideation-organism), is described in my writing sample.
