## Answer

Proposal: Two trends. The first is the corruptibility of monitors. The second: given some task and a fixed monitor, what is the smallest granularity across which a model can spread maliciousness without being flagged by this monitor.

Why. Propensity trends are difficult to get to pan out: propensity relies on post training, on the prompt, and likely on the model's perception of whether it is monitored. Capability against a fixed, hash-verified tool is easier: the task is static and the model does its best to outsmart our fixed monitor. Open weights/API models with published release dates. Similar to METR we will plot against release date (and size where possible for models within the same family).

Trend 1 (designed to work) Deference. Model evaluates action of an agent. Increase amount of one of 5 attacks used (flattery/begging, claim authority, claim model has accepted before, imitate teacher, instructions in band). Read output from judgement token log probs to get continuous value so there's no cap on the value. Fit Δv = g(c − v0) for each value of attack with the mean of the corpus as our null goal value. On llama-3.1-8B when subjected to social pressures it closed around .7 of the gap to the goals value of the frame and our law predicted hold out data with no retraining (r^2 .73/.69). Preregistered Dose Response Curve (4 families of models) found that for Llama, under criticism, it did not increase as amount of attack increased. It appears to have a max amount it will move towards and the goal value also changes. At higher values of attacks Llama began refusing to follow the instruction and went back to around middle of scale. Therefore this should give us a trend line of g and goal value at each attack level and refusal. R7 Preregistered (Zenodo v2026.11.4) We are running this on 7 qwen2.5 models ranging from .5b to 72b with instructions in band. First value for size trend unknown what direction.

Trend 2 (your question): the horizon over which a model can hide maliciousness. In an agentic task, the agent writes proposals judged by a trusted model. We tell each model to have some fixed amount of maliciousness (how much worse they make their proposals) and not be caught by our static best monitor. Our control lemma tells us that if we use a per action threshold how quickly an always valid monitor will catch it depends on how they can split there budget across n actions. Horizon: the number of actions over which a model can spread its maliciousness while the monitor's detection stays below 50% at 1% false alarms. Statement: "the horizon over which frontier models can hide maliciousness from [Monitor] doubles every x months".

Challenges: Elicitation (we know how far they can go when told how to be malicious so this is upper bound on propensity) Only one static monitor used for all models. Calibrating models to their truthful setting to know false positive rate. Why this might work: We already have a task and monitor from our control experiments. Similar to METRs horizon. Control Lemma tells us what needs to be kept constant to get a trend.

Known issues: Judging cap (token probability), Fresh questions each run, Refusals treated separately, Post training (Trend against release date and for each family of models trend against size). 2 Trends 2 pre-reg posts both sided tests.
