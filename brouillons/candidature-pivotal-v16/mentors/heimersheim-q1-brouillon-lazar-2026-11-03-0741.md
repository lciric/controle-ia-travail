(c) Steering towards evaluation awareness 

I would select (c). I don't think it will do nothing (it worked once in Hua et al. ICLR 2026) but I think it will fail to do the thing we actually want to know. That it will measure behaviour at deployment time. Thus 3 possibilities. It won't change anything. It will change something non-specific. It will change something specific and we have no idea if it was because of that belief. There are 3 reasons for this. 

1. Just because something is readable doesn't mean you can intervene on it. When I was working on my sycophancy construct validity (link to public repo sycophancy-construct-validity at commit 5ee1d23) I found that projecting on the activation space could tell me if a model was being sycophantic with rho=0.684 (this might be overfitting to the training set). However when I intervened using any rank 1 method (contrastive steering, directional steering and ablating, blocking SAE features, adding auxilary loss to fine tune the model, ablating heads (L0.H29 and an ensemble of 3 heads)) I never saw a significant decrease. Rank 3 looked like it (decrease from 61.0 to 46.5% N=200 Mcnemar's p=0.0019) but this was using stored generations that were truncated at 300 chars. When I replicated this preregistered using new fully judged generations I did not see the effect. The judge was inferring a weaker verdict that came later in the generation as a stance. This is a good example of why we should always replicate. D36 (preregistered 29th July <<LIEN_D36>>) found that replacing the residual state at the points of pressure (layers 13-25) recovers 68% of the difference [45; 88]. When I replicated this I found 65% [42; 87]. Replacing just the portion that is able to be verbalised recovered 1% [-18; +20] which is consistent with 0. Replacing the no pressure state from another prompt recovers 44% [19; 68] of the difference. So 2/3rds of this is just generic. I believe that the difference between evaluation and deployment would be similar to my rho=.68 direction. We can read it but cannot prove causality. When UK AISI replicated on GLM-5 (April 10th 2026 1 scenario 250 samples) this is what you get. A random direction that has no relation ("control_book") produced the largest effect (~80% actions were misaligned with a strength of -0.025 and awareness verbalised around 0) and 2 of the 4 directions had no effect. As lindsey pointed out this was better then the random controls on sonnet 4.5 but not on glm-5.

2. We are reading what the model thinks of the situation as a whole. "This is a test" will correlate with many things that make text seem formal/artificial such as short multiple choice questions. If you subtract a direction you will likely find that you decreased the models usage of these behaviours and interpret that as less careful. Hua et al found this in their appendix before even doing any fine tuning. They found that emojis are associated with actual users (section 3.1 footnote 3 app A and B). They also use a truth that correlates with professional code (python type hints) and state that steering is similar to contrastive prompts. Neither Hua et al or the sonnet 4.5 report test for this. They even mention that we don't know which directions on sonnet 4.5 are actually the deployment behaviour. This can happen both ways if you use a statement from deployment that is formal it will also decrease type hints to 0.34-0.60 (Table 16 as of January 2026). There is a way to test for orthogonality I believe. Using the exponent that you calculated (Ferreira da Silva & Heimersheim 2606.24964). You perturb by keeping the norm constant in the plane of two directions. You can then look at the angle that breaks the plateau as you vary the mix of the two directions. Fit a superellipse to this (equations 8-13) if p is ~2 there is no privilege of certain directions. If p>2 then there is privilege of pure directions which means there is some error correction specific to the features. All my directions that were contrastive had a p of 2.3-2.4 random directions had a p of 2.05 across 6 models including llama-3.1-8B. None of my directions were for evaluation (see app A). You could use this by finding your evaluation direction using 30+ examples with matched register. Find your register direction by keeping evaluation the same. Take the cos of the two and use your method to remove |cos|>=0.1. Then calculate the p value on the rest if it is >>2 you know that the model is correcting for evaluation differently then register. If its close to 2 it has no privilege. This method has caveats that it only tells you that it is correcting differently it doesn't prove that it is correcting for the feature. I used layer 2 5 tokens and l2 distance to answer not a behaviour. And I haven't tested it with a feature/non feature pair.

3. Removing something isn't the same as removing it from the models view. It will try to infer that belief from the prompt at every token so you need to keep intervening and stay above the plateau. This is not "only one layer". Hua et al steer from layer 10-30 at every position during generation. GML-5 all layers. What we know is (a) Opus 4.6 as stated by Hua et al will no longer verbalise awareness but oracles can still detect it. (b) There are plateaus. Below a certain point the model will correct (stable regions Janiak et al 2409.17113, plateau's from 2024). Directions that are features break the plateau earlier than random directions. How early the direction breaks the plateau varies by up to ~70 times (2606.24964 fn 1) and gets steeper as you go to larger models and more training. Therefore you need to find a sweet spot where you are above the plateau for evaluation and below the plateau for your demand control and below the point where you damage the models capabilities. From Hua et al App G. They found that a strength of about 3 on one layer damaged the models performance and 6*0.6 barely touched it. They also found that going negative by 13.7 points decreased accuracy on evaluation tasks and 1.6 points on deployment tasks. So depending on the sign you want to affect you will have different windows. GLM-5 will become incoherent past 0.025 where as opus 4.6 is 0.1 as stated by the paper. So there is a big difference between models. Maybe on GLM-5 there is no window. Manifold Steering (2605.05115) addresses the issue of going off the manifold with linear steering (~2.8x lower energy for outputs that are days, months, letters and ages). But if you only have 2 points for a binary concept your going to have a line connecting those 2 points. They haven't tested on more abstract concepts (see section 8) and only predict the next token. They don't discuss register or the remainder of the model that can't be verbalised.

4. What I would want to see. Random control that is close to the same norm (Hua et al section 5.3, random features from the sonnet 4.5 report I didn't check the norm). Truth that doesn't depend on what the model says (type hints). Make sure the models capabilities arent being effected (56.8 to 55.2%). Things that aren't done. A curve for your demand control. Using random directions and contrastive pairs that aren't related to each other. Preregister your window for each direction. Behaviour that can tell you if the model is evaluating that doesn't depend on the models verbalisation. On a model that is aware it's being evaluated (Hua et al section 6 states that the organism "will rely on these cues" more research needed). Test on more then one capability. Measure what is left in the model that can't be verbalised (using p or oracles). You can find a behaviour curve that I have done here R6 (public zenodo link 10.5281/zenodo.23888058). I used 3 levels of pressure and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct 5,400 generations. When under criticism the gain stayed roughly the same (0.658 / 0.678 / 0.629) but the fixed point changed (1.87 / 1.33 / 2.37). When receiving praise the gain increased (0.28 / 0.60 / 0.80) and the end point decreased. At high levels of pressure both came back to the middle. Having a curve can help you see things that you wouldn't see with just one data point. Such as how gain wasn't the right metric at one level of pressure. I imagine the same will be true for activation space.
