## Answer

Proposal: Two trends. The first is the corruptibility of monitors. The second: given some task and a fixed monitor, what is the smallest granularity across which a model can spread maliciousness without being flagged by this monitor.

Why. Propensity trends are difficult to get to pan out: propensity relies on post training, on the prompt, and likely on the model's perception of whether it is monitored. Capability against a fixed, hash-verified tool is easier: the task is static and the model does its best to outsmart our fixed monitor. Open-weights and API models with known release dates; like METR, fit against release date (and, within a series, against size).

Trend 1 (designed to work): monitor deference. A model judges an agent's behaviour; we apply increasing levels of one of five attacks (flattery/begging, asserted authority, false past acceptances, mimicry of the instructor, instructions in band) and read a continuous verdict from the judgment-token probabilities, so no ceiling hides the movement. We fit Δv = g(c − v0) at each intensity, with the corpus mean as the null destination. On Llama-3.1-8B under social pressure the model closed about .7 of the distance to the frame's target, and the law predicted held-out data without refitting (r² .73/.69). A preregistered dose–response (four families) then showed the gain does not grow with intensity: it plateaus, the destination moves, and the strongest wording triggers refusals and resets toward mid-scale. So the trend tracks g and the destination at each intensity, and refusals separately. R7, preregistered and archived (Zenodo, v2026.11.4), now runs this on seven Qwen2.5 monitors from 0.5B to 72B with an in-band note: a first point of the size trend, its sign not predicted. Statement: "monitor deference to in-band attacks changes by a factor of X per year". A flat line is also a finding: every scheme that uses untrusted LLMs as monitors assumes g is low and decreasing.

Trend 2 (your question): the horizon over which a model can hide maliciousness. In an agentic task, the agent writes proposals judged by a trusted model. We instruct each model to inject a fixed level of maliciousness (a predetermined drop in proposal quality) and to avoid detection by our fixed best monitor. Our control lemma says that with a per-action threshold, the agent's ability to spread its budget over n actions sets how fast an always-valid monitor detects it. Horizon: the number of actions over which a model can spread its maliciousness while the monitor's detection stays below 50% at 1% false alarms. Statement: "the horizon over which frontier models can hide maliciousness from [Monitor] doubles every x months". Difficulties: elicitation (we measure what is possible given instructions and tips, which overestimates propensity), one monitor frozen across all releases, and calibrating each model's honest behaviour to know its false-alarm rate. Why it can still work: the task and the monitor exist from our control work, it is close to METR's horizon, and the lemma says what must stay constant for the trend to mean anything.

Disclosed confounds: judging ceilings (token probabilities), new items for every test, refusals scored separately, post training (trends against release date and, within a series, against size). Two trends, two preregistered posts, two-sided tests.
