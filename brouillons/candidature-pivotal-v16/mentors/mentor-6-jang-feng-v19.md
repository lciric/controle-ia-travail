## Answer

White-box methods examine the computation underlying an action, rather than its presentation. The lack of whitebox access hurts black-box auditing: sandbagging by imitating a weaker model broke black-box detection in the auditing game (arXiv 2512.07810), probes fared better, and training-based elicitation exposed full capability. My motivation is architectural: if misbehavior is spread across many actions, it will necessarily blend in with noise on a per-action basis; you want something that they can't sanitize, that gives you per-action signal that you can calibrate on honest episodes and bank using an anytime-valid false alarm bound, and activations can work well for this. Moving a probe that reads the acting agent also requires you to move weights, not just context (arXiv 2609.36490), but I'll leave that as an aside that you take as conjecture instead of a result. Your exploration-hacking paper discusses how your organisms reason explicitly, making them susceptible to text monitors; the white-box problem gets real once they learn not to. Weight-based methods have this advantage over black-box.

Why you should care: White-boxing superpowers your auditor toolbelt.

Caveats:

1⃣ Readable isn't causal. Example: Found direction super cheap to monitor on Llama-3.1-8B that correlated with change in sycophantic judgement (ρ = .68) but accounted for <1% of causal effect, where 68% of the causal effect was attributable to the residual state overall. Monitoring said direction may correlate in distribution yet completely break when pushed/pwned.

2⃣ Construct validity: Reading hidden instruction, evaluation awareness, or a register is NOT the same as weaponized intent. This is your 'how does this organism get its disease' (organism etiology) question. This is why I always train probes on one organism + test on a different recipe setting (hidden instruction ≠ LoRA).

3⃣ Signal on real weights is... LIMITED! On my first try using real model weights (Llama-3.1-8B, 2,400 actions), saboteur behavior was easy to see with the naked eye, but a saboteur that cheated by inflating their reported cost was completely invisible to our generic probes, which failed to meaningfully surpass their random-probe baseline. In-sim, thresholds calibrated on fragments, not entire honest episodes, suffered from episode effects leading to false alarms 21-35% of the time instead of our target 5% rate.

4⃣ Adaptive attackers. Attackers who use prompt-level strategies are already nudging SAE- and activation-based methods towards confident incorrect responses (arXiv 2602.08877). Naïve probes lost to sandbaggers the red team had trained to beat them. Adversarial robustness must be evaluated against an attacker, never assumed.

5⃣ Training-based elicitation also amplifies non-saboteurs, so it will have false positives as a detector.

6⃣ Access: White-box requires access to weights. That means today it's an inside job. External accountability mechanisms will remain black-box for the foreseeable future.

Questions I'd want answered by any white-box claim: Baseline against the best black-box method you can find held to a fixed false alarm rate; what's your random probe floor? And please test your detector on an organism created by a different method than you used to train your probe. (Preferably all of the above preregistered.)
