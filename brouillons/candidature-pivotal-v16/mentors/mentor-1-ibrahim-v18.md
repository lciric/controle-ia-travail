## Q1 — Pick one of the two projects and briefly describe the first experiment you'd run (what you'd train or measure, expected result, one thing that could make it misleading)

Direction 1: if you put in a persona through character training does it remain after RL and if so how does that impact sycophancy.

Some things i would want to train/test. Using a open weight model with character training that can be replicated. I would then implement 1 rl step that pushes for higher warmth/agreeableness (we know from your warmth paper leads to higher sycophancy). I would measure levels of sycophancy pre and post the rl step. Using displacement laws i have created for llama-3.1-8b-instruct. When the model is put under social pressure its ratings change based on how far away from a constant they are. Δ=g*(c-p0). By a constant factor towards that constant. So the model will go a percentage of the way towards some constant when in a certain social frame (g = ~.7). There are 2 constants one when being criticized and one when praised. I was able to predict ratings using this displacement law without fitting to a new social pressure situation. (r2 of .73 and .69 out of sample). So i would look at if g or those constants changed with RL. If the model had a higher g and its constants moved closer to the users frame the persona has changed. I would also want to see if the model retains its concessions. From my study if you take away the model's concession it goes back further to its original judgement than if you take away the social pressure.

My prediction is that the model will become more sycophantic from RL. Higher g and move its constants. But it should not change the frame-set structure (two constants) because it's induced by the social frame.

Ways this could be false. The model may learn to rate different after RL. It could only appear more sycophantic because it's using the rating scale different. If you just compared ratings pre and post RL you would think the model was more sycophantic. I would test this by having conditions with no pressure as well. And seeing if g is greater than it should be from regression to the mean. One concern is from your construct validity paper is that my llm judge is in the same family as my model. So it may be biased towards my model. I would test this by having a sealed set rated by humans.

## Q2 — Describe a technical project where you trained or evaluated LLMs

"Sycophancy as Attraction Toward a Fixed Point" (manuscript in preparation; code + data public: github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law). I used measured social pressure and psychophysical methodology (2 campaigns, ordinal LLM judge blinded prior to seeing any data, permuted nulls, history removal + length-matched placebos to identify the pressure effect over the model's natural yielding) to attempt to gain scientific insight into how Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct may be affected. We observed that sycophancy acts as a quantity displaced, not simply if or how often. Linear to starting score: the model will move ~0.7 of the distance towards some goal set by the social frame on average. That some highly rated items could be lowered in score with praise (we were able to predict this). And that our first campaign was able to make accurate predictions on the second campaign (R² of .73/.69), with no new parameters fitted. Using 60 new items written blind and hashed before any generation, the first-campaign law with no refit reached an R² of .68 on criticism, .58 on praise, when compared to a static shift at −.28/−.01. Using 2 open-weights judges from another family we can observe both pulls to the two goals: Qwen2.5-7B identified them on a sample of 360 readings, Qwen2.5-72B on all 1 200 responses (margin between the two goals of .76). Without any judge, the model's own 1–5 rating shows the attraction under criticism (g = 0.63, R² = 0.91); under praise it is weak, and the two-destination criterion does not survive a change of inference implementation. Two-sided attraction is specific to Llama-3.1-8B: Qwen2.5-7B (as preregistered) and Mistral-7B-v0.3 show uniform shifts, while Gemma-2-9B shows strong attraction under criticism only.

To see what inside the model carries this behaviour, I ran the causal arm on the same model system: a probe direction moderately correlated with the behaviour (ρ = 0.68) but accounting for <1% of the causal effect versus 68% for the full residual state vector; six rank-1 "knobs" well established to causally control LLM behaviour failed to shift judged behaviour (steering, directional ablation, SAE clamping, fine-tuning against the probe direction, zeroing a model head/MLP); then shifting along a rank-3 SVD subspace altered judged behaviour; finally putting the model in a zero-pressure control allowed us to verify this shift was generic. The predictions, evaluation and interpretation rules for each of these studies were preregistered + hash-sealed before observing data; all study engineering was performed by AI coding agents playing by written rules; and every number quoted in the paper can be traced to its source file + hash, proving it has not been changed since preregistration.
