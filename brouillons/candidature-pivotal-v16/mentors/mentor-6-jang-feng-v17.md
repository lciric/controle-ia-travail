## Answer

Summary: White-box methods analyse the computation responsible for an action, not how it is presented. Black-box auditing suffers because of this: in the sandbagging auditing game (arXiv 2512.07810) black-box detection was circumvented by mimicking a weaker model while probes showed more promise and training-based elicitation retrieved full capability. My incentive is architectural: misbehavior that is distributed thinly across lots of actions is, by design, indistinguishable from noise when looked at on a per-action basis; you want something that they can't sanitize that gives you per-action signal that you can calibrate using honest episodes and then bank under an anytime-valid false alarm bound, and activations could work nicely for this. Moving a probe that reads the acting agent also seems to require write access to weights, not just context (arXiv 2609.36490), though I take that as a conjecture to be tested rather than a result. Your exploration-hacking paper points out the organisms it used reason explicitly, making them easy for text monitors; the white-box problem becomes acute when they learn not to. Training-based methods also act on the weights (fine-tuning to elicit, noise on weights), giving you leverage no black-box technique has.
Why should you care: White-boxing massively increases your toolbelt as an auditor.
Caveats: 1⃣ Readable isn't causal. Example: I discovered one direction was cheap to monitor on Llama-3.1-8B that correlated with shift in sycophantic judgement (ρ = 0.68) but contributed to <1% of the causal effect, compared to 68% for the residual state overall. A monitor reading said direction may correlate ∈ distribution but break when pushed/pwned. 2⃣ Construct validity: Reading e.g. hidden instruction, evaluation awareness, or a register ≠ weaponized intent. This is your organism-etiology question. This is why I train my probes on one organism + test on a different recipe (hidden instruction vs LoRA). 3⃣ Signal on real activations is... limited! On my first try on real activations (Llama-3.1-8B, 2,400 actions), while the behaviour of a saboteur that cheated by raising its declared cost was plain to the eye, no generic probe cleared its random-probe floor (a bounded null: it rules out large shifts, not small ones). In simulation, using thresholds not calibrated on whole honest episodes led to false alarms 21-35% of the time under episode effects instead of the nominal 5%. 4⃣ Adaptive attackers. Prompt-level strategies are already nudging SAE- and activation-based auditors towards confident incorrect responses (arXiv 2602.08877). Naïve probes lost to sandbaggers trained by the red team. Adversarial robustness must be evaluated against an attacker, never assumed. 5⃣ Training-based elicitation also amplifies non-saboteurs, so it has false positives as a detector. 6⃣ Access: White-box requires access to weights. That means today it's an inside job. External accountability mechanisms will remain black-box for the foreseeable future.
Questions I'd want answered by any white-box claim: Baseline against the best black-box method you can find held to a fixed false alarm rate; what's your random probe floor? And please test your detector on an organism created by a different method than you used to train your probe. (Preferably all of the above preregistered.)
