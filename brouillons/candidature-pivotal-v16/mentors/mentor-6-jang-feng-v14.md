## Answer

Value. White-box methods read the computation behind an action rather than its presentation, and that is where black-box auditing breaks: in the sandbagging auditing game (arXiv 2512.07810), black-box detection was defeated by imitation of a weaker model, while probes showed more promise and training-based elicitation recovered full performance. My own reason is structural: sabotage spread thinly over many actions is invisible to a per-action monitor by construction; you need a per-action signal that can be calibrated on honest episodes and accumulated with an anytime-valid false-alarm bound, and activations give one the agent cannot fully curate. Moving a probe that reads the acting agent also seems to require write access to weights rather than context alone (arXiv 2609.36490), which I treat as a hypothesis to test, not a result. Your exploration-hacking paper notes that its organisms reason explicitly, which makes them easy for text monitors; the white-box question becomes live exactly when they stop. And training-based tools (fine-tuning to elicit, noise on weights) give a lever no black-box method has.

Limitations. (1) Readable is not causal. On Llama-3.1-8B I found a direction that tracks sycophantic judgement shift (ρ = 0.68) yet carries under 1% of the causal effect, against 68% for the full residual state. A monitor reading such a direction can work in distribution and fail under shift or attack. (2) Construct validity. A probe may read the hidden instruction, evaluation awareness or register rather than intent; this is your organism-etiology question, and the reason I never train and test probes on the same organism (hidden instruction vs LoRA). (3) Power and calibration on real data. In my first real test (Llama-3.1-8B, 2,400 actions), a saboteur that inflated a declared cost was plain in its behaviour, yet no generic probe cleared its random-probe floor; in simulation, thresholds not calibrated on whole honest episodes gave 21–35% false alarms instead of 5% under episode effects. (4) Adaptive attackers. Prompt-level strategies already push SAE- and activation-based auditors into confident wrong answers (arXiv 2602.08877), and naive probes were vulnerable to red-team behaviours in the sandbagging game; robustness has to be measured against an attacker, not assumed. (5) Training-based elicitation also lifts benign models, so as a detector it has false positives. (6) Access: white-box needs the weights, so today it is an in-house tool; external auditors are mostly black-box.

What I would ask of any white-box claim: a preregistered comparison against the best black-box baseline at a fixed false-alarm rate, a random-probe floor, and a detector tested on an organism built by a different recipe.
