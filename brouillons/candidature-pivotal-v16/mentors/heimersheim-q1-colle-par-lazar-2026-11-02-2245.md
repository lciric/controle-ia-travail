c) Anti-steerability for eval awareness. 

Subtract away any direction that communicates “someone is judging me”. I expect this one to fail the most of all seven interventions for three reasons, and because I already tried this exact intervention on another model and saw it fail.

Readable != causal. Found a direction in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct that correlated (ρ = 0.68) with eval judgement shift but was only accounting for <1% of the causal influence when compared to intervening on the residual state which accounted for 68%. Tried 6 total interventions that directly manipulated the rank 1 direction (steered towards/babble direction, ablated direction (projected direction out of activations), clamped on SAE features that turned on for the probe, finetuned against negative probe values, zeroed out heads, zeroed out mlps). None of them changed the judged behaviour of the model. Applied interventions across a rank-3 direction identified by PCA-ing activation differences at the decision layer was able to move the needle, but applying the exact same shift but with zero social pressure (words randomly drawn from a tokenizer rolling the die for each position) added also shifted verdicts: the shift was generic. A eval awareness vector identified through contrastive prompting is exactly the kind of target this creates: human readable, but not demonstrated to be causal. There are two options that can happen. Nothing happens. Or something happens but it’s so damned generic that you cant dissociate it from harming the models abilities outright. The later is easy to falsely present as success.

The signal is contaminated by task. “This is a test” is correlated with things that aren’t actually related to eval awareness: register (formal / artificial / multiple choice / short context). Imagine you found a direction that polarized eval/deployment-like prompts, great! Now ask yourself if that direction is purely reading if this is an eval setting or if it’s reading the register. Steer away from that direction and sure model output is going to shift towards more casual/ deployment like language. The description of that shift is purely subjective overlapp with “the model is just being less careful”. We need orthogonal method of readouting eval awareness vs register. But still, the model’s own verbal endorsement is not that readout. The difference between what it says and what it knows is the holy grail we’re trying to measure. Without some orthogonal readout mechanism like I described above this entire endeavor is impossible to prove wrong.

Subtraction is not removal. There are countless signals priming the model that this is true. During every exchange going into and out of the judgement layer. You subtract the awareness vector represented as an activation at one layer, the model will learn it downstream given enough context to infer it. Continuous intervention is required. 

Demonstrate results. Show dose response curves of the injected awareness vector vs a random control vector normalized to the same size but matching in all other regards. Show some behavioral downstream truth that can’t be answered by simply asking the model if you were able to change their belief. Show that the model steered to not have the belief doesn’t simply have lowered performance across the board. Something along those lines would satisfy me.
