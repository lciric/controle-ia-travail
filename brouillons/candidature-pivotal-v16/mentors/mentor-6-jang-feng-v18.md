## Answer

Summary: White-box methods analyse the computation responsible for an action, not how it is presented. Black-box auditing suffers because of this: in the sandbagging auditing game (arXiv 2512.07810) black-box detection was circumvented by mimicking a weaker model while probes showed more promise and training-based elicitation retrieved full capability. My incentive is architectural: misbehavior that is distributed thinly across lots of actions is, by design, indistinguishable from noise when looked at on a per-action basis; you want something that they can't sanitize that gives you per-action signal that you can calibrate using honest episodes and then bank under an anytime-valid false alarm bound, and activations could work nicely for this. Moving a probe that reads the acting agent also has to write to weights, not just context (arXiv 2609.36490), though I take that as a conjecture to be tested rather than a result. Your exploration-hacking paper points out the organisms used reason explicitly, making them easy for text monitors; the white-box problem becomes acute when they learn not to. Training-based methods also act on the weights, giving you leverage over any black-box technique.
Why should you care: White-boxing massively increases your toolbelt as an auditor.
Caveats: 1⃣ Readable isn't causal. Example: I discovered one direction was cheap to monitor on Llama-3.1-8B that correlated with shift in sycophantic judgement (ρ = 0.68) but contributed to <1% of the causal effect, compared to 68% of the causal effect for the residual state overall. A monitor reading said direction may correlate ∈ distribution but break when pushed/pwned. 2⃣ Construct validity: Reading e.g. hidden instruction, evaluation awareness, or a register ≠ weaponized intent. This is your organism-etiology question. This is why I always train my probes on one organism + test on a different recipe (hidden instruction vs LoRA). 3⃣ Signal on real weights is... limited! On my first try with actual model weights (Llama-3.1-8B, 2,400 actions), while saboteur behaviour was plain to the eye, a saboteur that cheated by raising their declared cost was completely invisible to generic probes that did not even clear their random-probe floor. In simulation, using thresholds not calibrated on whole honest episodes led to false alarms 21-35% of the time instead of the 5% rate we'd expect, under episode effects. 4⃣ Adaptive attackers. Attackers who use prompt-level strategies are already nudging SAE- and activation-based methods towards confident incorrect responses (arXiv 2602.08877). Naïve probes lost to sandbaggers the red team had trained to beat them. Adversarial robustness must be evaluated against an attacker, never assumed. 5⃣ Training-based elicitation also amplifies non-saboteurs, so it will have false positives as a detector. 6⃣ Access: White-box requires access to weights. That means today it's an inside job. External accountability mechanisms will remain black-box for the foreseeable future.
Questions I'd want answered by any white-box claim: Baseline against the best black-box method you can find held to a fixed false alarm rate; what's your random probe floor? And please test your detector on an organism created by a different method than you used to train your probe. (Preferably all of the above preregistered.)
