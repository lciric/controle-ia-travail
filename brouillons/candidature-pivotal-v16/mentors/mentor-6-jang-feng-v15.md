## Answer

Summary: White-box methods analyse the computation responsible for an action, not how it is presented. Black-box auditing suffers because of this: in the sandbagging auditing game (arXiv 2512.07810) black-box detection was circumvented by mimicking a weaker model while probes showed more promise and training-based elicitation retrieved full capability. My motivation is structural: sabotage applied thinly over many actions is by construction indistinguishable from noise to a per-action monitor; you want something they can't sanitize that produces a per-action signal you can calibrate on honest episodes and accumulate under an anytime-valid false alarm bound, and activations fit the bill. Moving a probe that reads the acting agent also seems to require write access to weights, not just context (arXiv 2609.36490), though I take that as a conjecture to be tested rather than a result. Your exploration-hacking paper points out the organisms it used reason explicitly, making them easy for text monitors; the white-box problem becomes acute when they learn not to. Training-based methods also act on the weights (fine-tuning to elicit, noise on weights), giving you leverage no black-box technique has.
Why it matters: Going white-box vastly expands an auditor's toolkit.
Limitations: 1️⃣ Readable isn't causal. I found a direction cheap to monitor on Llama-3.1-8B that correlates with sycophantic judgement shift (ρ = 0.68) yet accounts for less than 1% of the causal effect, versus 68% for residual state overall. A monitor that reads such a direction may correlate in distribution but fail when conditions shift or under attack. 2️⃣ Construct validity: Reading e.g. hidden instruction, evaluation awareness or a register ≠ malicious intent. This is your organism-etiology question. It's why I train probes on one organism then test on a different recipe (hidden instruction vs LoRA). 3️⃣ Signal on real activations is... sparse! On my first try on real activations (Llama-3.1-8B, 2,400 actions), while the behaviour of a saboteur that cheated by raising its declared cost was plain to the eye, no generic probe cleared its random-probe floor (a bounded null: it rules out large shifts, not small ones). In simulation, using thresholds not calibrated on whole honest episodes led to false alarms 21-35% of the time under episode effects instead of the nominal 5%. 4️⃣ Adaptive attackers. Prompt-level strategies are already nudging SAE- and activation-based auditors towards confident incorrect responses (arXiv 2602.08877). Naïve probes lost to sandbaggers trained by the red team. Adversarial robustness must be evaluated against an attacker, never assumed. 5️⃣ Training-based elicitation also amplifies non-saboteurs, so it has false positives as a detector. 6️⃣ Access: White-box requires access to weights. That means today it's an inside job. External accountability mechanisms will remain black-box for the foreseeable future.
Questions I'd want answered by any white-box claim: Baseline against the best black-box method you can find held to a fixed false alarm rate; what's your random probe floor? And please test your detector on an organism created by a different method than you used to train your probe. (Preferably all of the above preregistered.)
