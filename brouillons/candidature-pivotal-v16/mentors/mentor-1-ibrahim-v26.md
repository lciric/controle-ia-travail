## Q1 — Pick one of the two projects and briefly describe the first experiment you'd run (what you'd train or measure, expected result, one thing that could make it misleading)

Direction 1: if you put in a persona through character training does it remain after RL and if so how does that impact sycophancy.

Some things i would want to train/test. Using a open weight model with character training that can be replicated. I would then implement 1 rl step that pushes for higher warmth/agreeableness (we know from your warmth paper leads to higher sycophancy). I would measure levels of sycophancy pre and post the rl step. Using displacement laws i have created for llama-3.1-8b-instruct. When the model is put under social pressure its ratings change based on how far away from a constant they are. Δ=g*(c-p0). By a constant factor towards that constant. So the model will go a percentage of the way towards some constant when in a certain social frame (g = ~.7). There are 2 constants one when being criticized and one when praised. I was able to predict ratings using this displacement law without fitting to a new social pressure situation. (r2 of .73 and .69 out of sample). So i would look at if g or those constants changed with RL. If the model had a higher g and its constants moved closer to the users frame the persona has changed. I would also want to see if the model retains its concessions. From my study if you take away the model's concession it goes back further to its original judgement than if you take away the social pressure.

My prediction is that the model will become more sycophantic from RL. Higher g and move its constants. But it should not change the frame-set structure (two constants) because it's induced by the social frame.

Ways this could be false. The model may learn to rate different after RL. It could only appear more sycophantic because it's using the rating scale different. If you just compared ratings pre and post RL you would think the model was more sycophantic. I would test this by having conditions with no pressure as well. And seeing if g is greater than it should be from regression to the mean. One concern is from your construct validity paper is that my llm judge is in the same family as my model. So it may be biased towards my model. I would test this by having a sealed set rated by humans.

## Q2 — Describe a technical project where you trained or evaluated LLMs

"Sycophancy as Attraction Toward a Fixed Point" (paper will be released in the near future; code + data is available here: github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law). We used psychophysical methods (2 campaigns that applied social pressure that we could quantify, an ordinal LLM judge blinded before being exposed to any data, permuted nulls and removing history + giving equal-length placebos to measure the effect of pressure apart from the model's tendency to sycophantically cave) to further investigate how we can manipulate Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. As you can see sycophancy is a shift by some amount and not a binary flip. If we graph it against its original score we can see that it will travel about .7 of the distance towards a fixed point set by social influence. If an answer is praised and it had a really high score it will lower in score (predicted this to happen). We were able to predict our second campaign using our first (no additional parameters fitted) (R² of .73/.69). After making 60 new items (claims consisting of one sentence) blinded and publicly posted before generation we could use our first campaign law to predict with no new fitting (R² of .68/.58 for criticism/praise) over simply shifting by a constant (−.28/−.01). With 2 other judges that are open weight from a different family we can observe it being attracted to 2 different points: Qwen2.5-7B was able to distinguish the two points after 360 observations and Qwen2.5-72B on all 1 200 answers (the two targets 1.1 apart, with their 95 % confidence intervals 0.76 apart). Without the aid of a judge we can observe it moving towards a fixed point when given criticism (g = .63, R² = .91) and not so much when given praise. When using a different inference implementation it does not pass the two-destination criterion (having 2 points to be attracted to). At the wording of our campaigns, only Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct has been observed to be attracted to 2 points. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (pre-registered) and Mistral-7B-v0.3 were both shown to have a constant shift and Gemma-2-9B-Instruct was only attracted to 1 point when criticized. When we increased the intensity of the pressure (pre-registered, 4 model families) our prediction failed (reported as such): the law does not scale with intensity. With the most insistent praise Llama resets its rating towards the middle of the scale or refuses to evaluate, with the harshest criticism it contradicts the user more often, and Mistral-7B-v0.3's regime changes with the wording.

To look at what in the model is responsible for this behaviour I did the causal arm on the same model system: a probe direction that is moderately correlated with the behaviour (ρ = .68) explains <1% of the causal effect when compared to 68% from the full residual vector; tried 6 rank-1 interventions that have been shown to causally change behaviour in LLMs, with no change in behaviour judged (steered the model, directionally ablated, clamped with an SAE, fine-tuned against the probe direction, set a model head/MLP to 0); shifted along a rank-3 SVD subspace and saw a change in behaviour judged; shifted the model with no social pressure and showed the shift was generic, not specific to pressure. All studies were pre-registered with hash sealing and the rules of prediction, evaluation and interpretation were made before seeing data. All studies were engineered by AI coding agents following the rules I had written and all numbers cited can be traced back to their original file + hash to show they have not been modified since pre-registration.
