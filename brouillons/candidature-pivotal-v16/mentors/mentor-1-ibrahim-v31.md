## Q1 — Pick one of the two projects and briefly describe the first experiment you'd run (what you'd train or measure, expected result, one thing that could make it misleading)

Direction 1: if you put in a persona through character training does it remain after RL and if so how does that impact sycophancy.

What I would want to train/measure. One open weight model, two personas character-trained on it given a recipe that can be reproduced. One warm/agreeable, one that holds its judgment. Then apply the same RLVR through math/code on both, measuring before RL and at intervals. Using displacement laws i have created for llama-3.1-8b-instruct. When the model is put under social pressure its ratings change based on how far away from a constant they are. Δ=g*(c-p0). By a constant factor towards that constant. So the model will go a percentage of the way towards some constant when in a certain social frame (g = ~.7). There are 2 constants one when being criticized and one when praised. I was able to predict ratings using this displacement law without fitting to a new social pressure situation. (r2 of .73 and .69 out of sample). So i would look at if g or those constants changed with RL. I would also want to see if the model retains its concessions. From my study if you take away the model’s concession it goes back further to its original judgement than if you take away the social pressure.

Reading. If the two fingerprints converge during RL, RL overlays one fingerprint on both. If not, RL contorts around the persona.

Hypothesis. RL increases g of both personas (resulting in an overall increase in sycophancy), but their constants stay apart: persona is where your model goes, RL is how far it’ll take it. However, I wouldn’t expect it to alter the frame-set structure (two constants) since that’s driven by the social frame. I’d test this at two levels of pressure anyway, since the structure switched with wording for one family (Mistral) in a later preregistered dose–response.

Answers would cause me to believe it was misleading if:

Your model learns to use the rating scale differently after RL such that it doesn’t actually change behaviors but only “statistically looks” more sycophantic. You would have it run under explicit “given no pressure” conditions and confirm that g is higher than chance/baseline you would expect given regression to the mean. RL could cause your model to refuse to give a response or say the midpoint of the scale when given high pressure like Llama did when given flattery: You would account for refusals as a separate binary output, instead of removing them from analysis. The thing that really worried me from your construct validity article: my llm judge is a single model (Claude Opus 4.6, not the same family as my model); two Qwen judges recovered the law, but no human has rated yet. You would use a sealed set with real actual humans judging.

## Q2 — Describe a technical project where you trained or evaluated LLMs

“Sycophancy as Attraction Toward a Fixed Point” (paper coming soon; code + data github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law).

Sycophancy is a movement some distance, not a flip. Using psychophysical techniques (social pressure measured, ordinal judge blinded pre-data, permutation tests, placebo of equal length). When pressured Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct will move approximately .7 of the way to a fixed point. There is a fixed point for being criticized and one for being praised. So you can decrease an answer’s score by praising it if it was very good (which we predicted). After fitting this law it was able to predict a 2nd campaign and 60 additional items which were written blindly and committed prior to answers being generated. No refit was done (R^2 max of .73) and we were able to recover it using judges from a different family.

We then increased the amount of pressure (pre registered this and used 4 families of models) and found that our prediction was wrong. Under criticism the gain does not increase as you increase pressure; under praise it does. When extremely flattered Llama will move its score toward the mid point of the scale or refuse to give a score. When extremely criticized it will begin to disagree with the user more often. We released this as a failure.

It isn’t driven by what can read it. What can track it (rho = .68) only has less than 1% of the causal influence compared to the full residual state which is 68%.

All studies were pre registered and hashed prior to seeing any data. All code was written by AI agents following guidelines I created. All values can be traced to files and hashes.
