## Q1 — direction most likely to fail, and why

### Full version

(c) Steering against eval awareness.

Drive activation statistics away from those expected when a model believes it is being evaluated: subtract a direction describing "I am being tested". I expect this one to fail to hit its target most out of the seven, for three reasons, and I expect it because I already tried the same recipe on another construct and watched it fail.

Readable is not causal. I identified a direction on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct that correlates (ρ = 0.68) with sycophantic judgement shift but captures less than 1% of the causal effect, versus 68% for the full residual activation state. Running six rank-1 interventions against it (steering, directional ablation, SAE clamping, fine-tuning against the probe, head/MLP zeroing) did not change judged behaviour. Running interventions along a rank-3 subspace identified by SVD-ing activation differences at the decision point did move the needle, but applying the same shifts with no social pressure applied (a zero-pressure control condition) moved verdicts too: it was a generic shift. An eval awareness vector discovered via contrastive prompting is just the sort of target this represents: readable, not shown to be causal. The possible outcomes are that nothing happens, or that something happens but it is so generic it cannot be dissociated from harmful model degradation. The latter is trivial to misreport as successful.

The construct is polluted. "This is a test" correlates with things that are not the construct: register (formal / artificial / multiple choice / short context). If you find a direction contrasting eval-like and deployment-like prompts, it is reading the mixture. Steering away from it causes model output to become more informal/deployment-like, which is subjectively indistinguishable from "the model is behaving less carefully". An orthogonal readout of eval awareness vs register is needed, and again the model's verbal report is not that readout: what it says and what it knows is what we're trying to measure. Without something like that, the whole endeavour is unfalsifiable.

Subtracted is not gone. The model is exposed to many features that cue this belief across its entire context. If you subtract an activation vector representing the belief at one point, the model can re-derive it downstream. The intervention thus needs to be applied continuously, at a therapeutic dose. That dose may lie past the plateau in your question, where perturbations stop being neutral, and thus changing behaviour is no longer changing belief but hurting the model in some other way.

Things that would convince me: dose–response curves for the awareness vector against an otherwise norm-matched random control, some form of behavioural ground truth that the belief is present that isn't the model's self-report, and testing to show the steered-away model didn't just become generally degraded. That experiment, or one substantially similar, I would require to be convinced of a steering claim. That is also the experiment I would attempt myself.

### 150-word version

(c) Steering against evaluation awareness. Most likely failure: the direction that reads "I am being tested" is not the one doing the causal work. On Llama-3.1-8B I found exactly this for sycophancy: a direction with ρ = 0.68 to behaviour carried under 1% of the causal effect, against 68% for the full state; six rank-1 interventions did nothing, a rank-3 subspace moved behaviour, and a zero-pressure control showed that shift was generic. Second, the construct is contaminated: eval-like prompts also differ in register, so steering against the vector yields informal output that looks like "relaxed". Third, suppressed is not removed: the belief is re-derived downstream from many weak cues, so the dose needed may lie past the point where perturbations are neutral. A steering result would convince me with a dose–response curve, a norm-matched random control, and a belief readout that is not the model's own report.

## Q2 — why mechanistic interpretability (under 150 words)

One thing I want to know is how closely coupled what a probe reads is to what the model uses. A bunch of my academic background is in neuroscience, low-rank recurrent networks, and population dynamics – places where low dimensional readable structure and driving dynamics are two separable objects. The thing my read-out-of-sync result showed on Llama-3.1-8B (a direction that predicts sycophancy and does under 1% of the causal work) was evidence that that gap exists in a language model. Separately from why it exists, the gap matters for safety because activation monitors, steering defences, evaluation-awareness tests, etc. all fundamentally assume the gap is small. I want to draw that boundary for where it is and isn't small with preregistered interventions and controls, publishing null results along the way. Your plateau question is basically the cleanest instance of that measurement that I'm aware of: you're asking for a dose–response curve rather than the single-dose interventions my rank-1 vs rank-3 result amounted to.
