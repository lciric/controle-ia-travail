c) Steering against eval awareness.

Subtract away any direction that communicates “someone is judging me”. I expect this one to fail the most of all seven interventions for three reasons, and because I already tried the same recipe on another construct and saw it fail.

Readable != causal. Found a direction in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct that correlated (ρ = 0.68) with sycophancy judgement shift but was only accounting for <1% of the causal influence when compared to intervening on the residual state which accounted for 68%. Tried 6 total interventions that directly manipulated the rank 1 direction (steered towards direction, ablated direction (projected direction out of activations), clamped on SAE features that turned on for the probe, finetuned against negative probe values, zeroed out heads, zeroed out mlps). None of them changed the judged behaviour of the model. Applied interventions across a rank-3 subspace identified by SVD-ing activation differences at the decision point was able to move the needle, but applying the exact same shift but with zero social pressure (a zero-pressure control condition) also shifted verdicts: the shift was generic. An eval awareness vector identified through contrastive prompting is exactly the kind of target this creates: human readable, but not demonstrated to be causal. There are two options that can happen. Nothing happens. Or something happens but it’s so generic that you cant dissociate it from harming the models abilities outright. The later is easy to falsely present as success.

The signal is contaminated by register. “This is a test” is correlated with things that aren’t actually related to eval awareness: register (formal / artificial / multiple choice / short context). Imagine you found a direction that polarized eval/deployment-like prompts, great! Now ask yourself if that direction is purely reading if this is an eval setting or if it’s reading the register. Steer away from that direction and sure model output is going to shift towards more casual/ deployment like language. The description of that shift is purely subjective overlap with “the model is just being less careful”. We need orthogonal method of readouting eval awareness vs register. But still, the model’s own verbal endorsement is not that readout. The difference between what it says and what it knows is the holy grail we’re trying to measure. Without some orthogonal readout mechanism like I described above this entire endeavor is impossible to prove wrong.

Subtraction is not removal. There are countless signals priming the model that this is true. During every exchange going into and out of the judgement layer. You subtract the awareness vector represented as an activation at one layer, the model can infer it downstream given enough context. Continuous intervention is required.

Demonstrate results. Show dose response curves of the injected awareness vector vs a random control vector normalized to the same size but matching in all other regards. Show some behavioral downstream truth that can’t be answered by simply asking the model if you were able to change their belief. Pick an open problem in AI alignment you think is underrated. What experiment would you run first, and what result would change your mind? *

Underrated: a score that cannot be inflated by an agent that games it on purpose. Overrated: rejections based on resubmission-detectors that won’t work anymore when researchers start using automated alignment agents to optimize on your score automatically. With your automated alignment researchers, a method’s top score is the maximum of noisy evaluations, so it’s completely rational for an agent to just resubmit the same method repeatedly unchanged (which is what happens in 26 of the 39 detections you report), and your best score is an upwardly biased estimate of that method’s true value. You treat this as pathological misbehaviour we need better ways to detect in Monitoring, but it’s just the utility-maximizing reaction to how you’ve designed your score, and worse: as agents get better at seeking rewards (i.e. your future failure mode) the solution will have to come from changing your score, not detecting failure: don’t accept a method until the evidence it outperforms everything already accepted remains significant no matter how many times you re-evaluate it on fresh seeds, and still holds on benchmarks it hasn’t seen and on bigger models. Leaderboard overfitting in the presence of adaptive submissions is well understood (the Ladder, the reusable holdout); underrated is leaderboard overfitting by an agent who understands this.

Experiment 1: construct a judge that can’t be overfit to on your test harness via resubmissions. Start by rescoring your existing leaderboard methods on new seeds (how much is selection bias inflating your scores? ), then against new benchmarks/larger model (does it generalize at all?). Replace your “best of noisy runs” envelope with a sequential test: update a test martingale using each method’s evaluations against the null hypothesis of baseline + every method already accepted on your leaderboard (accommodating any mixture of accepted methods to carry 0 evidence), bound using Ville’s inequality to guarantee false positive rate of at most α no matter how many sequential tests you run, and a simple method-level multiple testing procedure. Retest your logged methods from their logged per-run scores, then evaluate new agents that have observed verdicts. Metrics: percent of flagged methods (and subsequent re-submissions) that still get accepted through; how much power we lose on true-progress methods (those that closed the gap, as judged by your held-out benchmark and Petri); how hard it is for a verdict-aware agent to search towards a class of methods your judge already declared outside of its coverage; all compared against-the-baseline of using the Ladder. Using my system, a model proposer did, both with and without verdicts: known laws repeatedly combined piecewise over sub-populations. The fix’s preregistered validation runs next.

Evidence that would sway me: empirical demonstrations that the power lost on truly novel methods exceeds some a priori chosen margin (the judge would be too blunt an instrument to bother deploying in research settings), agents being able to pass unchanged resubmissions at rates substantially higher than α (the null hypothesis is wrong), agents that see verdicts performing no better than blind resubmission at beating the naive maximum (the problem is less severe than I think), or top methods keeping their score on fresh seeds (little adaptive overfitting, as Roelofs et al. found on Kaggle competitions). Each of those are pre-registered analyses; either way, I’ll send you the paper.

What are your thoughts on the value of white-box monitors and white-box methods for auditing? What do you see as their current limitations? *

White-box methods analyze the computation behind an action, rather than how it’s presented. A lack of whitebox access degrades black-box auditing: sandbagging subverted black-box detection in the auditing game (arXiv 2512.07810), probes did better, and training-based elicitation revealed full capability. Motivation for me is architectural: if misbehavior is distributed across lots of actions, it will perforce appear noisy on a per-action basis; you want something they can’t sanitize , something that gives you per-action signal you can calibrate on honest episodes and bank with an anytime-valid false alarm bound, and activations can do this well. In arXiv 2609.36490, a probe reading the acting agent’s activations was moved with a rank-1 weight update, prompting context alone not being sufficient signal, but I’ll leave that up there as an aside that you take as conjecture rather than a result. Your exploration hacking paper mentions how frontier models can reason explicitly about suppressing exploration, which makes them vulnerable to text monitors; the white-box problem becomes acute once they learn not to. Weight-based approaches benefit from this over blackbox.

Caveats:

Readable does not mean causal. Running example: A direction that was trivially easy to monitor on Llama-3.1-8B correlated with shift in sycophantic behavior (ρ = .68) but captured <1% of the causal effect. When measured, 68% of the causal effect came from the full residual state. Just because something correlates on aggregate doesnt mean it won’t break when exploited.

Construct validity: Just because you can read hidden instruction / evaluation awareness / peek at a register doesnt mean that mechanic is there cause its weaponized. This is your “how does this organism get its disease” (organism etiology) question. Which is why my next test does not train probes on one organism and test on that organisms exact same process / process view (hidden instruction != LoRA)

Signal on raw activations is limited. While saboteur behavior was obvious to the naked eye when charting behavior in our first experiment with real model activations (llama-3.1-8B,2400 actions), a saboteur who was inflating their reported cost in strategically valid ways was not picked up at this sample size by our otherwise “generic” probes, failing to meaningfully exceed the performance of random probes. And in-sim we saw evidence thresholds not calibrated on full honest episodes (as they absolutely must be treated as if they need to be) had episode effects causing false alarms between 21-35% of the time rather than the nominal 5% false alarm rate.

Attackers can adapt: Prompt level attackers are already nailing SAE- and activation-based auditors to confidently wrong answers(arXiv 2602.08877). Naive probes were beaten by sandbaggers from the red team (arXiv 2512.07810). Any claim of robustness to adversarial attack must be tested against an attacker, trust but verify, it can never be assumed up front.

Because training based elicitation will amplify signals from non-saboteurs, it too will produce false positives as a detector.

Access: Whitebox requires access to weights. That means for now, it’s only something you can do from the inside. Any realistic external source of accountability is going to be blackbox for the foreseeable future.

I do realize these tests also apply to my own work: in a preregistered dose–response experiment measuring sycophancy (four model families, 30 items, K = 5), my prediction of the scaling of the displacement with intensity of pressure failed (outcome C), and is published as such, with an independent counter-reading and a secondary analysis dropping the refusals (the outcome remains C).

I’d want these answered by any white-box claim:

Baseline against the best black-box method you can find, held to a fixed false alarm rate, what’s your random probe floor?

Train your detector on an organism generated by one process, test it on an organism created by a different process than you used to train your probe. Ideally all of the above preregistered.

Direction 1: You imprint a persona through character training, does that carry over after RL? If yes, how does that impact sycophancy?

What I would test. Take 1 Open-weight model and Character train 2 personas on it with the exact same recipe + cookbook (Something that can be duplicated by others): One Warm/agreeable persona, One Personality that is stand-offish and doesn’t change its opinion easily. Then run the exact same RLVR(math/code) on both personas measuring before RL and at intervals during RL. I would measure sycophancy with displacement laws I have created for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. When the model is put under social pressure, its ratings move towards a constant by a constant fraction of their distance to it: Δ = g·(c − p0). So the model goes a percentage of the way towards a constant set by the social frame (g ≈ 0.7). There are 2 constants, one when being criticized and one when praised. I was able to predict ratings with this law without fitting to a new social pressure situation (r² of .73 and .69 out of sample). So each persona has a fingerprint (g and its two constants), and I would look at whether it changes with RL. I would also check whether the model retains its concessions: in my study, if you take away the model’s concession it goes back further to its original judgement than if you take away the social pressure.

Reading. If the two fingerprints come together during RL, RL stamps both fingerprints on top of each other. Otherwise RL wraps around the assigned persona’s fingerprint.

Hypothesis. RL will increase g for both personas (overall increase in sycophancy), but leave the two constants separated: Persona determines which “team” your model is on, RL determines how far it moves towards that team’s constant under pressure. I would not expect RL to change the existence of two constants (i.e. the frame-set structure) as that results from the framing of the social interaction itself. I would want to test this at 2 different levels of pressure anyways, as the frame-set “structure” changed (between attraction to the constants and a uniform shift) with wording for 1 family (Mistral) when I did a later separate preregistered dose–response study.

Describe a technical project where you trained or evaluated LLMs (feel free to link to code or any public documentation/write up if you have something to share). *

“Sycophancy as Attraction Toward a Fixed Point” (paper coming soon; code + data github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law).

Sycophancy is a movement some distance, not a flip. Using psychophysical techniques (social pressure measured, ordinal judge blinded pre-data, permutation tests, placebo of equal length). When pressured Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct will move approximately .7 of the way to a fixed point. There is a fixed point for being criticized and one for being praised. So you can decrease an answer’s score by praising it if it was very good (which we predicted). After fitting this law it was able to predict a 2nd campaign and 60 additional items which were written blindly and released prior to answers being generated. No refit was done (R^2 max of .73) and we were able to recover it using judges from a different family.

We then increased the amount of pressure (pre registered this and used 4 families of models) and found that our prediction was wrong. The law does not increase as you increase pressure. When extremely flattered Llama will move its score toward the mid point of the scale or refuse to give a score. When extremely criticized it will begin to disagree with the user more often. We released this as a failure.

It isn’t driven by what can read it. What can track it (rho = .68) only has less than 1% of the causal influence compared to the full residual state which is 68%.

All studies were pre registered and hashed prior to seeing any data. All code was written by AI agents following guidelines I created. All values can be traced to files and hashes.

Describe an ML experiment you ran end to end (fine-tuning, RL or evals). What did you find, and what would you do differently? (max 200 words) *

Attraction to a setpoint (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; code/data: github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law)

Pre-registered before data was collected; hash-locked the ordinal judge. Permutation nulls. Performed surgery on history and compared to a sham of the same length. All pre-registered and hash-locked.

When you're being sycophantic you're moving towards a set point, not randomly changing odds. It's proportional to distance from the set point. You will concede roughly 0.7 of the distance to a setpoint that depends on the type of social pressure. Criticism and Praise have different setpoints meaning something highly rated can be lowered by praise which happened. Law held up when testing campaign-1 fit on campaign-2. If we remove the model's conceding it goes back closer to the original judgement than removing the social pressure. A direction that tracks the behaviour (ρ = 0.68) explained <1% of the causal effect. The full residual state explained 68%.

What I would have done differently. Judges from another family (Qwen2.5-7B and 72B) have since recovered the law, but I would have had human labels from the start. Also would have looked into the law before and after character training/RL (your project B).
