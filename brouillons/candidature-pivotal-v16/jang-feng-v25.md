# Jang et Feng — v25 — texte de Lazar (22:28 UTC), fond corrigé

White-box methods analyze the computation behind an action, rather than how it’s presented. A lack of whitebox access degrades black-box auditing: sandbagging subverted black-box detection in the auditing game (arXiv 2512.07810), probes did better, and training-based elicitation revealed full capability. Motivation for me is architectural: if misbehavior is distributed across lots of actions, it will perforce appear noisy on a per-action basis; you want something they can’t sanitize , something that gives you per-action signal you can calibrate on honest episodes and bank with an anytime-valid false alarm bound, and activations can do this well. In arXiv 2609.36490, a probe reading the acting agent’s activations was moved with a rank-1 weight update, prompting context alone not being sufficient signal, but I’ll leave that up there as an aside that you take as conjecture rather than a result. Your exploration hacking paper mentions how frontier models can reason explicitly about suppressing exploration, which makes them vulnerable to text monitors; the white-box problem becomes acute once they learn not to. Weight-based approaches benefit from this over blackbox.

Caveats:

Readable does not mean causal. Running example: A direction that was trivially easy to monitor on Llama-3.1-8B correlated with shift in sycophantic behavior (ρ = .68) but captured <1% of the causal effect. When measured, 68% of the causal effect came from the full residual state. Just because something correlates on aggregate doesnt mean it won’t break when exploited.

Construct validity: Just because you can read hidden instruction / evaluation awareness / peek at a register doesnt mean that mechanic is there cause its weaponized. This is your “how does this organism get its disease” (organism etiology) question. Which is why my next test does not train probes on one organism and test on that organisms exact same process / process view (hidden instruction != LoRA)

Signal on raw activations is limited. While saboteur behavior was obvious to the naked eye when charting behavior in our first experiment with real model activations (llama-3.1-8B,2400 actions), a saboteur who was inflating their reported cost in strategically valid ways was not picked up at this sample size by our otherwise “generic” probes, failing to meaningfully exceed the performance of random probes. And in-sim we saw evidence thresholds not calibrated on full honest episodes (as they absolutely must be treated as if they need to be) had episode effects causing false alarms between 21-35% of the time rather than the nominal 5% false alarm rate.

Attackers can adapt: Prompt level attackers are already nailing SAE- and activation-based auditors to confidently wrong answers(arXiv 2602.08877). Naive probes were beaten by sandbaggers from the red team (arXiv 2512.07810). Any claim of robustness to adversarial attack must be tested against an attacker, trust but verify, it can never be assumed up front.

Because training based elicitation will amplify signals from non-saboteurs, it too will produce false positives as a detector.

Access: Whitebox requires access to weights. That means for now, it’s only something you can do from the inside. Any realistic external source of accountability is going to be blackbox for the foreseeable future.

I do realize these tests also apply to my own work: in a preregistered dose–response experiment measuring sycophancy (four model families, 30 items, K = 5), my prediction of the scaling of the displacement with intensity of pressure failed (outcome C), and is published as such, with an independent counter-reading and a secondary analysis dropping the refusals (the outcome remains C).

I’d want these answered by any white-box claim:

Baseline against the best black-box method you can find, held to a fixed false alarm rate, what’s your random probe floor?

Train your detector on an organism generated by one process, test it on an organism created by a different process than you used to train your probe. Ideally all of the above preregistered.
