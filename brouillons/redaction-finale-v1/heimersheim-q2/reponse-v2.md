I want to work on mechanistic interpretability because reading a behaviour did not let me control it. In Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, a probe direction tracked sycophancy (ρ = 0.684, possibly training fit), yet no single-direction intervention I tried reduced it, at the doses I tried (github.com/lciric/sycophancy-construct-validity). Your paper with Ferreira da Silva finds that contrastive directions, readable by construction, are privileged under your error-correction test (p > 2). My question is whether a direction the network privileges also controls the behaviour it predicts. If yes, your test could screen steering targets, such as an evaluation-awareness direction; if no, privilege and control need separate tests. I have preregistered both measurements on the directions that social pressure adds to the activations: your test, and whether rotating toward them moves the model's ratings (R9, github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law; not yet run).
