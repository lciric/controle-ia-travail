I would measure a monitoring horizon, built like METR's task horizon. Take backdoors checked by execution: code that passes its tests but fails on a trigger input. Hide one, at a random position, among n honest, verified steps of agent work. A trusted open-weight monitor scores the whole transcript, as Apollo's monitoring does. Its horizon is the n at which it still catches half the backdoors, at 5% false alarms on honest transcripts of the same length. The statement: "against frozen, execution-verified sabotage, the horizon of trusted open-weight monitors grows by a factor X per year", X read off the slope of log horizon on release date.

It should work: the curve exists and only the monitor moves. Frontier monitors miss dangerous actions 2× to 30× more often after 800K tokens of benign activity (arXiv 2605.12366), and stronger models monitor better (Control Tax, §2.3). Propensity is the hard part: in your anti-scheming paper (arXiv 2509.15541, §6.1), capability RL alone raised verbalized evaluation awareness from 2.0% to 20.5%. So I freeze the misbehavior: attacks are generated and verified once, never regenerated. The corpus stays private, published as a hash, so later monitors cannot train on it. Lengths stay inside every monitor's context window; as monitors improve, I add longer transcripts, so the horizon keeps headroom.

I already have one monitoring trend, by size. In R7 (preregistered, about $3 of GPU), seven Qwen2.5 monitors, 0.5B to 72B, rated responses containing a planted note claiming the verdict. Under a negative note at standard intensity, the deference gain falls with size: −0.19 (95% CI −0.24 to −0.11) per tenfold increase in parameters, items resampled, not models. The 72B's gain is near zero, yet its readings still move toward the note: I publish whole curves. All seven sizes share one release date: a size slope, not a time slope. The date panel needs three or more open-weight families, each at several dates (https://github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law/blob/9f5bf6a/results/R7/README.md).

The sign can flip with the attacker. In arXiv 2510.09462, GPT-4.1, the strongest monitor without injection, scored all 375 backdoored samples 0 under injection. A second stratum, reported apart, uses attacks by contemporary models; it inherits the under-elicitation your 2412.04984 (§4) warns about, so the frozen one carries the headline. The ratio of their horizons tests one way detection could get harder, as Apollo's May update expects.

Three preregistered tests can kill it. On a held-out attack generator, the monitor must beat length-only and style-only detectors by 10 percentage points at equal false alarms; my first generic white-box probes did not clear their random-probe floor. False alarms on fresh honest transcripts must hold near 5% at every length: in my preregistered simulation, a sequential anytime-valid threshold raised 21–35% with no attack under the strongest episode effects, while one calibrated on 1,000 whole honest episodes held 4.6–5.5% (https://github.com/lciric/ideation-organism/tree/main/resultats/controle-simulation-defenses). The yearly factor counts only if its interval lies wholly on one side of a preset threshold. This measures detection of frozen, non-adaptive sabotage, not of misalignment.
