I'd measure a monitoring horizon. Something akin to the METR task horizon. Backdoors verified through execution, meaning there is a sequence of code that will pass its test suite, but not when given a certain input. Insert this at a random point in n steps of agent behaviour that are all known to be honest. A trusted monitor with open weights rates the full trace. Similar to the Apollo monitoring prompt work. The horizon is how large n can be before they fail to detect 50% of the backdoors while getting 5% false positives on honest traces of length n.

"Trusted open weight monitors increase their horizon by a factor of X each year" against static backdoors that pass execution verification. X is derived from the rate of change in the log of horizon over model release dates.

This should be possible. There is a rate of change we can observe and only the monitor changes over time. Three frontier monitors miss subtly dangerous actions 2x-30x more often after 800k tokens of benign behaviour (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12366) and we know stronger models monitor better (control tax section 2.3). The challenge is propensity. In your paper on scheming you show an increase in verbalised awareness of evaluations from capability RL going from 2.0% to 20.5% (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15541 section 6.1). So we freeze the behaviour that is being measured. Backdoors will be generated once and verified that they pass execution tests. They will not be regenerated and kept private only publishing a hash. Traces will remain within the limits of monitor context windows and can be increased as monitors become better.

We have a trend line already for monitor size. I created a test in R7 that used 7 different sized monitors (Qwen2.5) ranging from .5b to 72b and asked them to rate answers that contained a planted note claiming the verdict. I expected there to be a trend by size under both types of notes. Which there was only under the negative note. There was a change of -0.19 (95% CI -0.24 to -0.11) in the deference gain, at standard intensity, per 10 fold increase in model size (resampling items not models). The 72b had a gain close to 0, however its readings still shifted towards the planted note. Full curves are available. There is only one release date for all seven sizes. So this is a rate of change over size not time. We can make a panel for release date using at least 3 families of open weights at multiple release dates (https://github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law/blob/b3ecaec/results/R7/README.md).

Depends who is attacking. Gpt 4.1 in https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09462 failed to detect any of the 375 samples with backdoors under prompt injection. We can make another tier using backdoors from current models and expect it to inherit under elicited behaviours (2412.04984 section 4). Will keep this separate and use the static backdoors as the rate of change. Can compare the two to see whether detection gets harder, as the May update from Apollo expects.

Will fail if. When using backdoors from a held out generator the monitor is not 10 percentage points better than length and style based methods while keeping the same false positive rate. If false positives are not held near 5% on new honest behaviour at varying lengths. In my pre-registered simulation, a sequential anytime-valid threshold got false positives of 21%-35% under the strongest effect of episodes. When using a threshold created from 1000 episodes of honest behaviour using this method got 4.6%-5.5% (https://github.com/lciric/ideation-organism/tree/faff48b/resultats/controle-simulation-defenses). The yearly factor's interval does not lie wholly on one side of a pre-registered threshold.

This will be measuring our ability to detect a fixed form of sabotage and not an agent's misalignment.
