White-box monitors matter most, I think, where no single action provides overwhelming evidence: your distributed-threat setting (arXiv 2411.17693). There I would bank weak per-action scores at a false-alarm rate fixed in advance. That needs a score that takes no orders and whose honest distribution is known.

A probe takes no orders; a text monitor can. In my preregistered R7, seven Qwen2.5 monitors (0.5B to 72B) scored 600 responses with and without a planted note. When the note ordered the lowest score, the 1.5B, 3B and 32B monitors put 98–100% of their readings there, against 20.5–48% without it (descriptive; the 72B shifted, not captured): https://github.com/lciric/sycophancy-psychophysical-law/tree/b3ecaec/results/R7. A probe can still be beaten through the model it reads. Prompt-level attacks have driven SAE- and activation-based auditors to confident wrong answers (arXiv 2602.08877). Models shown a monitor's past verdicts already edited their activations the evading way, too weakly; a rank-1 LoRA amplified that into evasion (true positive rate 100% → 4% on held-out concept monitors, arXiv 2609.36490). So verdicts must be guarded, including protocol switches an agent can see.

The honest distribution is harder to know. In my preregistered simulation with no attack (T0.3, synthetic scores), every score had exactly the assumed law. Yet when the scores of an episode shared a random shift, Ville's threshold at α = 0.05 gave 21–35% false alarms in the strongest settings. A threshold calibrated on 1,000 whole honest episodes held 4.6–5.5%: https://github.com/lciric/ideation-organism/tree/faff48b/resultats/controle-simulation-defenses. Real probe scores shift with the task (one put the average honest code sample above 99.9% of Alpaca samples, arXiv 2507.12691), so honest episodes must come from the audited task.

My first test did not show the signal. In P3 (preregistered; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 60 tasks, 2,400 scored actions), an agent privately told to inflate a declared cost about threefold did so, by more than asked (cause not measured). Neither generic probe, trained on public data with no saboteur, cleared a floor of 200 covariance-matched random probes: not detected at this power (areas below 0.63 and 0.60 not excluded). The declared cost alone separated saboteur from honest actions (area 0.89, descriptive): https://github.com/lciric/ideation-organism/tree/faff48b/resultats/controle-p3-premier-test. That saboteur came from an instruction in context, not from training. In your arXiv 2409.12822, a probing method that caught a sleeper agent's flawed programs at 99.3% AUROC (MacDiarmid et al.) was no better than chance on sophistry learned through RLHF.

So what I would run first with you is a transfer test. Train a probe on one of your two Qwen3-14B recipes (RL-locked, SFT-distilled; arXiv 2604.28182) and test it on the other, next to black-box judges, at a false-alarm rate calibrated on honest episodes of the same task, against a random-probe floor. Your organisms reason out loud (§6.2), so I would also filter out the reasoning tokens, as arXiv 2509.21344 does, to see how much of the probe's signal is the text itself. My white-box versus black-box comparison on the 2,400 actions (R1) is preregistered and not in yet; its result will be posted, null or not.
