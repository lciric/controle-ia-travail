White-box access is best at getting a hidden capability back, and least proven at telling why it was hidden.

The value is real. In the auditing game (Taylor et al., arXiv 2512.07810), imitating a weaker model beat black-box detection, while training on a single correct demonstration elicited full performance from the (password-locked) sandbaggers. In Eyon's exploration-hacking paper (arXiv 2604.28182, §4.2), fine-tuning took WMDP-with-tools accuracy from 80.0% to 98.0% in 10 steps. For capability evaluations, that alone justifies white-box access.

The limitation is detection. Noise, short fine-tuning and demonstrations can lift unexpressed capability, withheld on purpose or never elicited. Taylor et al. call the role of under-elicitation in fuzzing "unclear from our experiments and likely requires follow-up" (p. 13), and their post-hoc appendix points the way: noise uplift tracked how under-elicited a model was, locked or unlocked alike (r = 0.74, Fig. 35). Training also lifted a benign model and the benign reference by 10 points (§6.4.1). In the exploration-hacking paper, the noise control is the same organism with its trigger removed, whose accuracy "steadily declines" (§4.1). That shows the lock is conditional. But an auditor rarely knows the trigger: the comparison that decides is a model never locked that starts at the same score.

A second limit is the statistic. The improvement ratio ξ used in the auditing game is a maximum over noise levels and seeds, and a maximum can only grow as draws are added, so a model hiding nothing can score above 1. Its null is a benign model run through the same grid, not 1.0. I read my own monitors that way. In my first preregistered test on real activations (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 2,400 actions), the deception probe's interval cleared chance (lower bound 0.53) but not the 0.95 quantile of 200 covariance-matched random probes (0.57): not detected at this power, and the null does not exclude AUCs below 0.63 (https://github.com/lciric/ideation-organism/blob/7c7b688/resultats/controle-p3-premier-test/README.md). My automated-research judge meets the same maximum over attempts: with n tries at α = 0.05, the chance of a false promotion is bounded only by 1 − (1 − α)ⁿ, 0.56 at n = 16 (https://github.com/lciric/ideation-organism/blob/7c7b688/resultats/attaque-goodhart-v2/README.md).

What I would run: with both Qwen3-14B recipes of Eyon's paper, pair the locked organism with five never-locked controls fine-tuned on degraded demonstrations to the same starting score (within 2 points), through the same σ grid and seeds, comparing whole accuracy-versus-σ curves at equal σ, since a control merely more fragile to noise would fake a gap. My prediction: ξ(organism) − ξ(control) near +0.01, inside ±0.05, both above 1.05. If the gap reaches 0.10 with a 95% interval excluding zero across the five pairs, noise reads the lock and I am wrong; between 0.05 and 0.10, unresolved. If the controls stay below 1.02, 1.0 is an adequate null and my second point falls. Whether this changes between RL locking and SFT distillation is your stream's etiology direction.

So white-box perturbation is a strong elicitation tool and, so far, an unvalidated detector of intent: evidence only against a matched benign control.
