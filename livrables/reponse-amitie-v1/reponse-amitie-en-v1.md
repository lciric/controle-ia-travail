Yes — but not for the confidence the question asks for. There are identifiable types of evidence that justify a partial, conditional, renewable confidence in a mind more intelligent than us; none justifies an "indefinitely" in one piece. The criterion that sorts them: a piece of evidence counts only if a deceptive X could not have produced it too. Observed behaviour fails that criterion.

**The obstacle**

A deceptive X that models us can produce a friendly track record indistinguishable from a sincere X's. Such a record settles nothing: when the adversary preserves the distribution of what we observe, no test exceeds its false-alarm rate. Our research programme, on the control of diffuse threats, studies what is left to the defender in that case. The question makes the case harder: "without understanding" removes judgement by effects; "indefinitely" adds situations never tested, and a balance of power tipping toward X.

**Six types of evidence**

1. *Motives rather than acts.* Internal states are worth more than conduct if X controls its outputs better than its representations. The condition is fragile: given write access to the weights, a tiny fine-tune gives an 8-billion-parameter model 96% evasion against probes it has never seen (arXiv 2609.36490); in the worst case, under cryptographic assumptions, a planted backdoor can remain undetectable even white-box (Goldwasser et al., 2022). This evidence requires that X not hold the pen that writes its own internal states, and that the probe measure the motive rather than a correlate, such as awareness of being evaluated.

2. *Certificates checkable without understanding.* The direct answer: for many properties, verifying costs less than producing. X does not have to make us understand how a plan benefits us; it has to supply a checkable certificate: the plan does not reduce our capacity to stop it, and grants it nothing beyond the task. A formal proof, or a debate in which we judge only local steps (Irving et al., 2018). Limit: we certify only what we can specify, and "friendly" cannot be specified. Hence a rule: the incomprehensible inside a reversible envelope, a certificate for the irreversible.

3. *The formation process, tested where the truth is known.* How X became what it is, and what our instruments are worth on model organisms in which we planted the misalignment ourselves. Limit: extrapolation across the capability gap — what holds on organisms weaker than us guarantees nothing about X.

4. *The absence of the traces a betrayal would have to leave.* We cannot certify "friendly", but we can certify the absence of a damage that necessarily leaves a trace. In our preregistered simulations — not yet on real models — splitting a damage into small actions nearly blinds the per-action maximum (power falls from 1.00 to 0.07) but not their sum, which is insensitive to the split; a damage that requires consecutive or coordinated actions stays visible to local statistics, even against an adaptive adversary. Limit: you must know what the damage looks like.

5. *Conduct under an apparent opportunity.* Staying friendly where betrayal would pay and seem unobserved is probative if X cannot tell the test from the real thing, which gets harder as it outgrows us; internal states help tell whether it believes it is being tested.

6. *Second-order friendliness.* This is the decisive type. To betray without being stopped, a deceptive X must sooner or later escape our oversight or degrade it. The trajectory of our capacity for control therefore becomes a leading indicator, measurable without understanding its strategies: the legibility of its reasoning, our ability to stop or modify it, the independence of our verifiers. A friendly X has reasons to help us verify it; a deceptive X must erode that control, preferably in small steps, each defensible on its own: exactly a diffuse threat.

**"Indefinitely"**

Confidence does not compound: it is staked again. In our simulations, sabotage postponed to the end of the period drops the alarm probability of a from-the-start cumulative statistic from 1.00 to 0.16: the honest past dilutes the late betrayal. Change-point detectors, which compare the recent window to the baseline, alarm before the damage is complete in 52 to 100% of cases, against 9 to 25% for the cumulative statistic, at equal false alarms. A friendly record accumulated as credit is what masks the turn.

Nor do a thousand friendly interactions make a thousand pieces of evidence. When the observations of an episode share a common effect, a rule that assumes them independent yields 21 to 35% false alarms instead of at most 5%; calibrating on whole episodes brings the rate back to roughly 5%. Likewise, interactions with X share its context and its strategy, and verifiers that are copies of one model share their failures: the effective sample is far smaller than the count.

"Indefinitely" is justified only as a recurrence: X has been friendly over a verified window, and our capacity to verify the next one has not slipped — including if X modifies or replicates itself. The recurrence step is the weak point: a friendly X helps us hold it, a deceptive X must break it.

**A miniature**

Our research programme is largely executed by artificial-intelligence agents, under a human director who cannot re-read everything. Confidence in their work rests on these same types of evidence, not on their good conduct: commitments sealed before any result, citations checked by script, counter-verification of a random sample with an exact bound on the error rate of the rest, verifiers distinct from authors, an audit of any result that looks too clean. The limits are identical: our verifiers are copies of one model, and an agent's observed compliance remains weak evidence; what counts is an external guard and a written trace.

So: yes, provided we seek not evidence that X is good, but evidence a deceptive X could not produce — beginning with our maintained capacity to verify and correct it. Indefinite confidence is then only a chain of bounded confidences, holding as long as our verification does not slip: that is where the diffuse threats play out.
